# OMNII Constitutional Being

**Status:** Constitutional Doctrine  
**Layer:** Becoming / Existence  
**Canonical authority:** OMNII Canonical Architecture

## 1. Purpose

OMNII Constitutional Being defines what it means for an entity to exist as a constitutionally recognizable participant in the ecosystem.

Being is not merely a database record. It is the coherent identity, state, capabilities, relationships, provenance, and context through which an entity can be recognized as itself.

## 2. Core Definition

> **Being is the constitutionally coherent condition of an entity as identifiable, situated, related, stateful, and capable of participation in an ecosystem.**

## 3. Being Invariants

### 3.1 Identity Invariant

An entity must have a canonical identity or identifiable basis appropriate to its kind.

### 3.2 Continuity Invariant

Identity must remain traceable through legitimate state changes.

### 3.3 State Invariant

The system must distinguish an entity's current state from its identity.

### 3.4 Context Invariant

An entity exists within relevant relationships, territory, time, and ecological context without being reducible to any one of them.

### 3.5 Relationship Invariant

Material relationships must be representable without collapsing the identity of the entity into its relationships.

### 3.6 Capability Invariant

Capabilities should be distinguishable from permissions and authority.

### 3.7 Provenance Invariant

Material identity and state transitions must retain sufficient provenance to support continuity and accountability.

### 3.8 Plurality Invariant

Different kinds of entities may coexist without requiring a single identity model to erase their distinctions.

## 4. Entity Classes

The constitutional model may represent:

- people;
- families;
- organizations;
- institutions;
- businesses;
- communities;
- territories;
- physical assets;
- digital entities;
- AI agents;
- knowledge objects;
- services;
- ecological entities;
- composite entities.

This is an extensible classification, not a closed ontology.

## 5. Being State

A constitutional being may be represented through:

```text
Identity
  + State
  + Capabilities
  + Relationships
  + Territory / Context
  + Provenance
  + Lifecycle
```

The representation must avoid confusing the record of an entity with the entity's total reality.

## 6. Being and Continuity

Being provides the identity anchor required for continuity.

```text
Being₀
  ↓ state transition
Being₁
  ↓ state transition
Being₂
```

Change does not necessarily create a new entity. The constitutional system must determine whether a transition represents state change, transformation, succession, composition, dissolution, or creation according to applicable identity semantics.

## 7. Being and Becoming

Being is the present constitutional condition; Becoming describes transformation through time.

```text
Being
  ↓
Becoming
  ↓
New state of Being
```

The distinction prevents the ecosystem from treating identity as static while also preventing every change from erasing identity.

## 8. Being and Authority

Being does not automatically create authority. An entity may exist without having authority over another entity or domain.

## 9. Being and Consent

Being does not imply consent. The existence of an entity does not constitute agreement to participate in every ecosystem process.

## 10. Being and ABBA

ABBA may represent, reason about, and coordinate entities, but representation is not creation of the entity's constitutional identity.

ABBA must preserve uncertainty where identity cannot be established rather than inventing identity.

## 11. Being and OMNI

OMNI is the omnidirectional movement state. Being identifies the participant or condition from which movement occurs.

```text
OMNII = canonical architecture
Being  = constitutional existence
OMNI   = omnidirectional movement state
```

## 12. Failure Conditions

Being failure includes:

- duplicate canonical identities without reconciliation;
- identity changing without provenance;
- state being mistaken for identity;
- capability being mistaken for authority;
- presence being mistaken for consent;
- relationships replacing entity identity;
- historical continuity being destroyed;
- AI-generated assumptions being treated as established identity without evidence.

## 13. Constitutional Dependencies

Being depends on:

- Architecture
- Semantics
- Continuity
- Relationship
- Territory where context is material

Being provides the foundation for Becoming and Civilization.

## 14. Final Principle

> **To be is to remain constitutionally recognizable through change without being reduced to a record, relationship, or current state.**
