# OMNII — Global Verification & Trust Intelligence Capability

**Status:** CANONICAL CAPABILITY SCOPE  
**Scope:** Global-by-default  
**Layer:** Shared ecosystem verification, evidence evaluation and trust intelligence

## 1. Constitutional definition

**Global Verification & Trust Intelligence** is the reusable ecosystem capability for evaluating whether a claimed identity, capability, condition, credential, transaction history, document, asset, provider or relationship is supported by sufficient evidence for a declared purpose.

It answers:

> **“What is claimed, what evidence supports it, what has actually been verified, for what scope and purpose, by whom, when, and what remains uncertain?”**

Verification is not an abstract permanent label. Trust is contextual, evidence-based and time-sensitive.

## 2. Global-by-default

Verification can operate across:

```text
local → national → regional → continental → global
```

Jurisdiction-specific authorities, registries and legal requirements are incorporated through adapters and policy rather than separate trust ontologies.

## 3. Verification universe

The capability can evaluate claims involving:

- identity
- organization existence
- ownership/control claims where lawfully available
- address/location
- licenses
- certifications
- product specifications
- factory capability
- machinery condition
- capacity claims
- financial capacity claims where appropriately authorized
- professional qualifications
- service capability
- provenance/origin
- transaction history
- shipment events
- inspection results
- quality claims
- sustainability claims
- references
- contractual performance

## 4. Claim model

Represent:

```text
claim
claimant
subject
scope
purpose
supporting evidence
source
verifier
verification method
verification date
expiry
confidence
exceptions
contradictions
status
```

A verification record must not silently change the underlying claim.

## 5. Evidence hierarchy

Evidence may include:

- primary authority records
- signed documents
- independent inspection
- laboratory results
- registry records
- transaction evidence
- shipment evidence
- direct observation
- participant attestations
- trusted partner evidence
- historical performance
- public evidence
- machine/sensor evidence where permitted

Evidence strength is contextual rather than a universal single ranking.

## 6. Verification states

Use explicit states such as:

```text
UNVERIFIED
CLAIMED
EVIDENCE SUBMITTED
PARTIALLY VERIFIED
VERIFIED
VERIFIED FOR PURPOSE
EXPIRED
DISPUTED
REVOKED
SUSPENDED
UNAVAILABLE
```

“Verified” must always have a scope, method and date.

## 7. Trust as a contextual model

Trust intelligence may consider:

- evidence strength
- recency
- consistency
- independent corroboration
- prior performance
- transaction history
- claims/disputes
- capability fit
- regulatory standing where applicable
- chain-of-custody
- relationship history
- failure patterns

Trust is not equivalent to legality, and neither is equivalent to quality or suitability.

## 8. Supplier and factory verification

For production sourcing, verification may cover:

```text
identity
site existence
production process
machinery
capacity
sample capability
quality controls
certifications
export capability
performance
```

A directory listing is discovery evidence, not proof of capability.

## 9. Product and quality verification

Support verification of:

- specification conformity
- quantity
- dimensions
- materials
- test results
- packaging
- labeling
- batch/lot
- condition
- authenticity where verifiable
- acceptance criteria

Inspection and certification remain performed by the appropriate authorized parties.

## 10. Document and credential verification

The capability can validate document metadata, issuing authority, consistency, expiry and evidence relationships without becoming the issuing authority.

It should preserve originals or references where lawfully permissible and record verification outcomes separately.

## 11. Transaction-backed trust

Trust can be strengthened by observed outcomes:

- on-time delivery
- quality acceptance
- defect/return rate
- dispute frequency
- payment performance
- fulfillment accuracy
- documentation quality
- responsiveness

Performance is contextual: a supplier can be excellent for one capability and poor for another.

## 12. Contradiction and fraud signals

Detect or surface:

- inconsistent identities
- duplicate entities
- conflicting certifications
- suspicious document patterns
- impossible capacity claims
- review manipulation
- synthetic activity
- inconsistent locations
- repeated failed transactions
- materially changed business claims

Signals trigger further verification; they are not automatically proof of misconduct.

## 13. Continuous verification

Verification should support re-checking when:

```text
credential expires
supplier changes
capacity changes
new evidence appears
performance declines
regulation changes
material transaction occurs
```

The latest state must not erase historical verification history.

## 14. Verification packages

A reusable verification package can contain:

```text
subject
claims reviewed
evidence set
checks performed
results
exceptions
confidence
scope
verifier
validity period
next review
```

This package can be consumed by Sourcing, Opportunity, Matching, Finance, Insurance, TIP and institutional workflows.

## 15. Trust portability

A verified fact may be reused across ecosystem products only within its permitted scope and validity.

A supplier verified for one factory capability is not automatically verified for unrelated products or jurisdictions.

## 16. Governance and authority

The ecosystem may orchestrate verification and evidence collection but does not assume the legal authority of:

- regulators
- banks
- insurers
- certification bodies
- customs authorities
- courts
- professional licensing bodies

Where formal authority is required, the result is recorded as a regulated handoff.

## 17. Privacy and confidentiality

Verification must respect:

- consent
- purpose limitation
- lawful basis
- access control
- confidential commercial information
- data minimization
- retention requirements

Trust intelligence is not a license for unrestricted surveillance.

## 18. Ecosystem interfaces

Consumes/contributes to:

- Identity / Human Authority
- Evidence / Provenance
- Standards & Conformance Registry
- Provider Capability Registry
- Transaction Reputation
- Global Sourcing
- Global Opportunity
- Global Matching & Discovery Intelligence
- Global Research Intelligence
- TIP / IO
- Claims/Disputes
- Compliance
- Pulse
- ABBA

## 19. Non-goals

This capability does not become:

- a universal legal certification authority
- a credit bureau by default
- a regulator
- a substitute for professional inspection
- an immutable permanent reputation score
- an unrestricted surveillance system

## 20. Canonical invariant

> **Trust is evidence for a purpose, bounded by scope and time. Verification strengthens confidence without pretending to create authority.**
