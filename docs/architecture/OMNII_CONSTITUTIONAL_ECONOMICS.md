# OMNII — Constitutional Economics Doctrine

**Status:** Canonical constitutional doctrine supplement
**Scope:** Entire OMNII ecosystem
**Relationship:** Extends `CARBON_ACTUAL_ECONOMIC_ONTOLOGY.md` and `CARBON_ACTUAL_VALUE_PULSE_ENGINE.md`; does not create competing economic primitives.

## 1. Purpose

The OMNII economy is a value-centered ecosystem economy. Money, price and financial instruments may participate in economic activity, but they are not the ultimate measure of value or progression.

The economic system is designed to measure, preserve, circulate, develop and amplify value across people, capabilities, knowledge, assets, organizations, infrastructure, opportunities and outcomes.

## 2. Primary Economic Law

> **Value precedes money. Money is one representation and routing mechanism for value; it is not the definition of value.**

The ecosystem therefore measures value through multidimensional evidence and feedback before reducing any part of it to monetary terms.

## 3. Inverted Economics

OMNII intentionally inverts conventional economic perspective around stewardship and responsibility.

### Conventional intuition

```text
ASSET → something possessed
LIABILITY → something owed
WEALTH → accumulation
PROGRESSION → often correlated with financial position
```

### OMNII perspective

```text
ASSET → capability + responsibility + stewardship obligation
LIABILITY → unmet need + unrealized potential + opportunity for ecosystem intervention
WEALTH → accumulated and demonstrated value enabled
PROGRESSION → demonstrated contribution and value creation recognized through Pulse
```

The inversion is conceptual and constitutional. It does not erase legal accounting, debt, ownership, tax, contractual obligations or jurisdiction-specific financial law.

## 4. Assets as Responsibilities

An asset may create ecosystem responsibility because it requires:

- maintenance;
- stewardship;
- management;
- protection;
- lifecycle care;
- infrastructure;
- knowledge;
- energy;
- compliance;
- responsible use.

More possession may therefore mean more responsibility, not automatically more economic rank.

## 5. Liabilities as Potential

An unresolved liability can reveal:

- unmet need;
- unused capability;
- disconnected capacity;
- unfinished work;
- abandoned opportunity;
- undeveloped idea;
- maintenance deficit;
- knowledge gap;
- social or infrastructure gap.

The ecosystem should seek to **identify, curate, match, develop, resolve, recover or intentionally retire** such conditions rather than simply externalizing them.

## 6. Nothing of Value Is Lost

The ecosystem should preserve potentially valuable signals before requiring them to be fully formed.

A signal may begin as:

- a word;
- a name;
- an observation;
- a question;
- a contradiction;
- a glimpse;
- a frustration;
- a possibility;
- a hypothesis;
- an incomplete idea.

The ecosystem should preserve the original signal, context and provenance and allow later curation without rewriting history.

## 7. Actual → Becoming → Atlas

The economic progression of potential follows three ecosystem states:

```text
ACTUAL
  ↓
BECOMING
  ↓
ATLAS
```

### Actual

The raw, lived, captured or observed state: work performed, experience accumulated, thoughts generated, capabilities possessed, realities encountered and signals produced.

### Becoming

The ecosystem development state in which potential is curated and matched with:

- capabilities;
- people;
- mentors;
- networks;
- research;
- tools;
- infrastructure;
- opportunities;
- capital/financial capacity where appropriate;
- governance;
- partnerships;
- build capacity;
- consulting;
- training;
- scaling support.

### Atlas

The discoverable representation of what has become sufficiently formed to present to the ecosystem and wider world: capabilities, projects, ventures, achievements, organizations, opportunities, evidence and other realized or presentable states.

Atlas is therefore an outcome/discovery surface, not merely a profile page.

## 8. Ecosystem Stewardship of Potential

> **Potential entering the ecosystem becomes an ecosystem stewardship concern until its next state is known.**

The originating person or entity remains associated with provenance and authorship where applicable, but the burden of development does not have to remain entirely on that originator.

The ecosystem can distribute a potential across appropriate capabilities and institutions without transferring authorship or rights without an explicit canonical relationship.

## 9. Curation as an Economic Function

Curation converts unmanaged potential into organized possibility.

```text
SIGNAL
 ↓
PRESERVE
 ↓
CONNECT
 ↓
CURATE
 ↓
VALIDATE
 ↓
MATCH CAPABILITY
 ↓
MATCH NETWORK
 ↓
MATCH OPPORTUNITY
 ↓
BUILD / DEVELOP
 ↓
SCALE / HANDOFF
 ↓
ATLAS
```

Curation itself is value-creating work and should be measurable through Pulse.

## 10. Value

Value remains multidimensional. Relevant dimensions may include:

- knowledge;
- teaching;
- capability created;
- human effort;
- time;
- intelligence/compute;
- energy;
- data;
- attention;
- access;
- reliability;
- quality;
- opportunity enabled;
- harm prevented;
- maintenance performed;
- infrastructure enabled;
- social/community effect;
- environmental effect;
- continuity preserved;
- future capability unlocked.

The evaluator must preserve provenance, unit, context, confidence and time.

## 11. Pulse

Pulse is ecosystem feedback/evidence about what happened after contribution, interaction, learning, teaching, service, creation, protection, maintenance, coordination or other activity.

Pulse is not inherently praise, popularity, money, a star rating or a universal scalar.

Pulse may be derived from:

- use;
- successful completion;
- outcomes;
- downstream adoption;
- capability creation;
- quality;
- reliability;
- retention;
- repeat use;
- network effects;
- evidence of improved outcomes;
- harmful outcomes or failures;
- independent verification;
- contextual feedback.

## 12. Progression

Progression is primarily recognized from demonstrated contribution and value created, with Pulse serving as evidence/feedback.

Conceptually:

```text
CONTRIBUTION
 ↓
OUTCOME / ENABLED VALUE
 ↓
PULSE
 ↓
CURATION / EVALUATION
 ↓
PROGRESSION
```

Progression must not be reducible to financial wealth alone.

## 13. Teaching and Multiplicative Value

Teaching, mentoring and enabling others can create multiplicative ecosystem value because a contribution may continue through the capabilities of people who received it.

A teacher's value may therefore include:

```text
Knowledge transmitted
 + Capability created
 + People enabled
 + Downstream outcomes
 + Future value unlocked
```

The system should attribute downstream value carefully, avoid double counting, preserve provenance and distinguish contribution from sole causation.

## 14. Value Is Not Ownership

An entity can create substantial ecosystem value without owning substantial financial assets.

Conversely, an entity can hold substantial assets while producing limited, harmful or negative net ecosystem outcomes.

Economic rank should therefore not be inferred from possession alone.

## 15. Pulse Quality

A high-quality Pulse signal should identify:

- source;
- context;
- timestamp;
- method;
- evidence quality;
- confidence;
- affected entities;
- direct vs downstream effect;
- positive, neutral or harmful consequence;
- potential confounders.

The system must resist gaming through artificial engagement, manipulation, popularity contests, self-report inflation or coordinated false feedback.

## 16. Economic Circulation

The ecosystem aims for value circulation rather than passive accumulation.

```text
CAPABILITY
 ↓
CONTRIBUTION
 ↓
VALUE
 ↓
PULSE
 ↓
RECOGNITION
 ↓
PROGRESSION / ACCESS / SUPPORT
 ↓
MORE CAPABILITY
 ↓
MORE VALUE
```

This is the ecosystem economic flywheel.

## 17. Financing Is a Capability, Not the Definition of Opportunity

When a potential reaches a buildable state, the ecosystem can curate it toward:

- internal capability;
- partner capability;
- mentorship;
- consulting;
- grants;
- financing;
- investment;
- procurement;
- infrastructure;
- distribution;
- customers;
- research;
- institutional support;
- workforce.

Financing is therefore one possible enabling input among many.

## 18. Product Participation

Every OMNII product contributes to and consumes the same economic substrate.

Examples:

- HAPI develops human capability;
- Charter enables movement;
- Fleet coordinates asset operations;
- Logistico orchestrates goods movement;
- NAB preserves registry/provenance/state;
- Becoming curates potential;
- Atlas exposes discoverable outcomes;
- IO handles value movement and economic settlement;
- ABBA coordinates intelligence and ecosystem decision support.

No product creates a competing economic ontology.

## 19. IO Boundary

IO remains the canonical value-movement domain.

Other products may:

- declare requirements;
- expose obligations;
- register entitlements/rights references;
- report value evidence;
- request value operations;

but actual money/value transfer, settlement and economic ledger execution remain in IO.

## 20. No-Waste Economic Law

Useful residuals should not be discarded merely because their first workflow ended.

Residuals may include:

- unused capacity;
- idle assets;
- unrecognized skills;
- failed experiments;
- data residuals;
- energy residuals;
- unfulfilled opportunities;
- unfinished ideas;
- reusable materials;
- inactive networks.

The ecosystem should route them through curation, recovery, reuse, repair, repurpose, recycling or intentional retirement.

## 21. Safeguards

The inverted economic doctrine must not be used to:

- erase legal rights;
- seize property;
- conceal debt;
- manipulate people through a fabricated Pulse score;
- deny due process;
- turn popularity into entitlement;
- expose sensitive data;
- replace lawful accounting;
- create discriminatory progression mechanisms.

The doctrine is a value architecture; implementation remains subject to law, consent, evidence and governance.

## 22. Core Laws

1. **Value precedes money.**
2. **Assets imply stewardship.**
3. **Liabilities reveal potential or responsibility.**
4. **Nothing of potential value should be accidentally lost.**
5. **Preservation precedes interpretation.**
6. **Curation is economic work.**
7. **Contribution creates progression opportunities.**
8. **Pulse is feedback, not the whole of Value.**
9. **Teaching and enabling others can create multiplicative value.**
10. **Every product participates in the shared value ecology.**
11. **IO is the canonical value-movement boundary.**
12. **The ecosystem optimizes for value creation, continuity and becoming rather than accumulation alone.**

## 23. Constitutional Statement

> **The OMNII economy exists to recognize, preserve, circulate and multiply value wherever it appears. It does not define wealth by possession alone. It treats assets as responsibilities, liabilities as potential and unmet need as an opportunity for stewardship. It preserves emerging potential, curates it through Becoming, exposes realized capability through Atlas, and uses evidence and Pulse to recognize contribution and progression. Money participates in the economy; Value is the larger object.**
