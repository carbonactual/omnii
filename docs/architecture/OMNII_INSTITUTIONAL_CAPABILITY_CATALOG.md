# OMNII Institutional Capability Catalog

**Status: CANONICAL ARCHITECTURAL CATALOG**
**Purpose:** Define the reusable institutional capabilities from which government, private, nonprofit, community and other organizational systems can be composed.

## 1. Core rule

OMNII is a capability fabric, not a factory for one-off applications.

`BUILD ONCE → REGISTER → VERIFY → VERSION → COMPOSE → DEPLOY → MEASURE → STRENGTHEN`

A new engagement searches this catalog and the capability/swarm registries before creating anything new.

## 2. Capability classification

Every significant implementation item is classified as one of:

`constitutional/core | reusable capability | domain module | institutional configuration | integration adapter | presentation/UI`

A recurring capability must be promoted into the shared fabric when evidence shows that its semantics are useful across multiple domains.

## 3. Institutional capability families

### A. Identity, authority and trust

Capabilities: identity, authentication, authorization, roles, delegation, consent, attestation, verification, credentials, signatures, approval, organizational membership, authority scopes, revocation, recovery and identity lifecycle.

Institutional uses: staff and citizen accounts, director approvals, delegated signing, supplier verification, membership, access control and trusted interactions.

### B. Organization and institution management

Capabilities: institution profile, mandate, structure, office/unit hierarchy, role catalog, membership, committees, boards, reporting lines, responsibilities, policies, institutional memory, succession and continuity.

Uses: ministries, regulators, companies, universities, NGOs, associations, cooperatives, foundations and professional bodies.

### C. Registry and master data

Capabilities: registry engine, identifiers, registration, validation, deduplication, search, indexing, master-data reconciliation, lifecycle, lineage, cross-registry links, import/export and registry projections.

Examples: person, business, supplier, employee, student, patient, vehicle, property, facility, licence, product, asset, seed variety, contract, project, service and beneficiary registries.

### D. Schema, forms and intake

Capabilities: form builder, dynamic schema, conditional questions, drafts, attachments, validation, calculations, declarations, signatures, accessibility, multilingual content, offline capture, kiosk/mobile capture, assisted submission, versioning and form analytics.

A form is an executable interaction contract, not just a page.

### E. Documents, records and evidence

Capabilities: document intake, records management, indexing, classification, retention, versioning, evidence packages, OCR adapters, signatures, redaction, disclosure, archives, provenance and controlled sharing.

### F. Workflow and case management

Capabilities: workflow definitions, states, transitions, queues, tasks, cases, dependencies, approvals, reviews, decisions, escalations, parallel work, timers, SLAs, exception handling, retries, compensation, closure, reopen and audit.

### G. Work management and execution

Capabilities: work orders, assignments, dispatch, checklists, field work, inspection visits, service jobs, human execution, machine execution, agent execution, physical execution, proof of execution, completion and handoff.

### H. Automation and rules

Automation modes: event-driven, scheduled, time-window, threshold, rule/policy-driven, SLA-driven, queue-driven, data-change-driven, integration-driven and approved predictive/recommendation automation.

Controls: preconditions, permission checks, idempotency, dry-run, human approval gates, retry, timeout, rollback/compensation, quarantine, escalation, manual override, audit and kill switch.

Automation may execute within authority; it does not create authority.

### I. Decision and approval

Capabilities: decision requests, evidence packs, eligibility, policy evaluation, scoring, recommendation, approval chains, delegated authority, conflict checks, decision records, reasons, expiry, review and appeal.

High-consequence decisions must preserve the legally or institutionally required human boundary.

### J. Scheduling, capacity and availability

Capabilities: calendar, shift, rota, appointment, booking, reservations, capacity, resource allocation, availability, waitlist, recurrence, blackout periods, conflict detection and utilization measurement.

### K. Service delivery and customer/citizen experience

Capabilities: service catalog, request intake, self-service, assisted service, status tracking, queue management, case communication, FAQs/knowledge, service feedback and escalation.

### L. Licensing, permits and certification

Capabilities: application, eligibility, supporting evidence, inspection, testing, review, approval/rejection, fee assessment, issuance, digital credential, renewal, suspension, revocation, appeals and public verification.

### M. Inspection, audit and assurance

Capabilities: inspection plans, field checklists, sampling, observations, photographs, measurements, findings, corrective actions, audit trails, evidence, reinspection, assurance reports and risk-based scheduling.

### N. Procurement and sourcing

Capabilities: demand/requirements, supplier registry, sourcing, RFQ/RFP, bid intake, evaluation, approvals, award, purchase orders, contracts, delivery, acceptance, invoice matching, supplier performance and closeout.

### O. Contracts and commitments

Capabilities: agreement templates, parties, terms, obligations, milestones, conditions, signatures, amendments, renewals, breach, disputes, notices, settlement and contract lifecycle.

### P. Finance, payments and value

Capabilities: pricing, quotes, invoices, receivables, payables, collections, refunds, reconciliations, budgets, allocations, grants, expense management, settlement, ledgers, value measurement and economic reporting.

Regulated financial functions remain subject to applicable licensing and regulated-provider boundaries.

### Q. Assets, inventory and maintenance

Capabilities: asset registry, ownership/custody, inventory, stock movements, serials, inspections, condition, depreciation, spare parts, work orders, maintenance plans, warranties, disposal and lifecycle history.

### R. Projects and programmes

Capabilities: project charter, milestones, dependencies, budgets, resources, tasks, vendors, risks, deliverables, change control, progress, evidence, benefits realization and closeout.

### S. Supply chain, logistics and traceability

Capabilities: sourcing, orders, inventory, warehouses, transport, routing, dispatch, tracking, chain-of-custody, batch/lot traceability, proof of delivery, exceptions, recalls and settlement.

### T. Communication and notification

Capabilities: email, SMS, messaging, letters, digital notices, templates, subscriptions, routing, localization, delivery status, retries, preference management and official correspondence.

### U. Search, knowledge and institutional memory

Capabilities: full-text search, structured search, knowledge base, taxonomy, glossary, document discovery, semantic retrieval, memory, lessons learned, provenance-aware answers and knowledge lifecycle.

### V. Analytics, measurement and observability

Capabilities: KPIs, dashboards, operational analytics, cohorting, trends, anomaly detection, service-level measurement, utilization, outcome measurement, Pulse, Value, telemetry and performance evidence.

### W. Risk, security, privacy and resilience

Capabilities: risk register, controls, threat detection, security events, privacy impact, data classification, retention, access review, vulnerability workflows, supplier risk, incident response, business continuity, disaster recovery, quarantine and recovery.

### X. Compliance and policy management

Capabilities: law/policy catalog, obligations, control mapping, attestations, compliance workflows, exceptions, evidence, reviews, corrective actions, regulatory reporting and change monitoring.

### Y. AI, agents and intelligence

Capabilities: governed agents, prompts/instructions, tool permissions, model/provider adapters, memory boundaries, evaluation, routing, human handoff, task execution, recommendations, summarization, classification, extraction and monitoring.

Agents inherit identity, authority, policy, audit, safety and provenance boundaries.

### Z. Integration and interoperability

Capabilities: API gateway, webhooks, events, queues, file exchange, identity federation, data-sharing contracts, adapters, transformation, synchronization, reconciliation, rate limits, health checks, versioning and migration.

Provider implementations remain behind replaceable adapters.

### AA. Territory, geo and physical context

Capabilities: countries, states, LGAs, districts, sites, coordinates, routes, geofences, service areas, facilities, field locations, address normalization and map-linked operations.

### AB. Human/mobile/offline execution

Capabilities: mobile work, offline forms, local caching, sync, assisted-service desks, kiosks, field devices, GPS, camera/photo evidence, scanners, device health and operator queues.

### AC. Marketplace and exchange

Capabilities: listings, offers, discovery, matching, quotes, orders, reservations, contracts, payments, fulfillment, reputation, dispute and settlement.

### AD. Communication, participation and engagement

Capabilities: surveys, consultations, voting where legally/constitutionally appropriate, feedback, complaints, petitions, community participation, stakeholder meetings and response tracking.

### AE. Planning and simulation

Capabilities: scenario modeling, capacity planning, demand forecasting, route simulation, resource optimization, contingency plans, what-if analysis and pre-deployment testing.

### AF. Localization and accessibility

Capabilities: language, local policy variants, local calendars, currency, units, accessibility, assisted interaction and cultural presentation without changing universal semantics.

### AG. Continuity, migration and handoff

Capabilities: backup, restore, export, migration, version compatibility, archival, succession, operator handover, deployment packaging, documentation and exit plans.

### AH. Physical infrastructure and facilities

Capabilities: facility registry, rooms, sites, utilities, equipment, maintenance, occupancy, inspections, access, safety, energy and physical incident management.

### AI. Workforce and human capability

Capabilities: workforce registry, skills, credentials, availability, recruitment, onboarding, training, scheduling, performance evidence, succession, replacement and workforce planning.

### AJ. Public administration and civic operations

Capabilities: permits, revenue collection, public requests, notices, inspections, grants, benefits, licensing, public works, local administration, service directories and citizen feedback.

### AK. Research, laboratory and scientific operations

Capabilities: specimen/sample registry, chain-of-custody, tests, instruments, methods, results, quality controls, researchers, publications, approvals and evidence.

## 5. Universal execution lifecycle

```text
REQUEST / INTENT
→ OUTCOME
→ REQUIREMENTS / CONSTRAINTS
→ IDENTITY / AUTHORITY
→ REGISTRY RESOLUTION
→ FORM / SCHEMA
→ VALIDATION
→ WORKFLOW / CASE
→ TASK ROUTING
→ AUTOMATION / POLICY
→ TEAM / RESOURCE SELECTION
→ HUMAN / AI / MACHINE / PHYSICAL EXECUTION
→ STATE TRANSITION
→ EVENT + EVIDENCE
→ RECONCILIATION
→ VALUE / PULSE
→ LEDGER / SETTLEMENT where applicable
→ REPORTING
→ FEEDBACK
→ CURATION
→ IMPROVEMENT
```

## 6. Failure and exception contract

No consequential execution may depend on “happy path only.” Every executable composition should define:

`timeout | retry | idempotency | compensation | escalation | manual takeover | quarantine | dependency failure | partial completion | duplicate request | inconsistent state | rollback/migration | recovery | audit`

Unknown conditions remain visible until classified.

## 7. Reuse threshold

When the same operational pattern appears in two or more materially different domains, evaluate it for shared capability status. When a capability is universal, strengthen it centrally. When semantics remain domain-specific, keep the domain module separate while reusing universal engines underneath it.

## 8. Institutional deployment package

A deployable institution should be expressible as:

`core version + capability modules + domain packs + institutional configuration + registries/data + policies/rules + workflows/forms + integrations + UI + deployment controls`

This package must be exportable, versioned and independently operable, with clear ownership of data, configuration and authority.

## 9. Research basis

The catalog is consistent with current public-sector and enterprise platform patterns. OECD Digital Government Outlook 2026 identifies shared digital infrastructure, core registries, digital identity, payments, notifications, interoperability and reusable building blocks as foundational to joined-up services. World Bank GTMI 2025 measures core government systems, online service delivery, citizen engagement and GovTech enablers, including public financial management, tax, customs, HR/payroll, e-procurement and public investment systems. GOV.UK explicitly promotes common reusable platforms such as Forms, Notify and Pay. NIST risk-management guidance spans public and private organizations and includes cybersecurity, privacy and supply-chain risk; ISO 37301 applies compliance-management systems across public, private and not-for-profit organizations.
