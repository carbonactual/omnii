# OMNII UNIVERSAL MOVEMENT

## Status

Constitutional scope lock for the OMNII movement/transport substrate.

This document establishes the universal boundary: movement is the invariant; transport products are implementations and configurations of that invariant.

## Core Principle

OMNII models and orchestrates legitimate movement without making today's vehicle, geography, infrastructure, or technology the permanent boundary of the system.

> **Universal Movement = Subject + Purpose + Need + Want + Capability + Capacity + Resource + Route/Trajectory + Environment + Context + Signals + Services + Rules + Time + Pulse**

The system determines, curates, composes, coordinates, executes, monitors, adapts and learns from movement.

## Scope

Universal Movement accommodates, where lawful and technically meaningful:

- people, families, groups, passengers and operators;
- cars, buses, vans, trucks, trailers, motorcycles, bicycles and specialised vehicles;
- tow trucks, recovery vehicles, ambulances and other emergency/service vehicles;
- cargo, parcels, luggage, equipment, machinery and other things;
- animals and other living movement requiring specialised welfare and regulatory constraints;
- public, private, commercial, institutional, community and social movement;
- human-operated, remotely operated, autonomous and future transportation systems;
- ground, road, rail, water, air, orbital, lunar, planetary and other environments;
- spacecraft, space stations, lunar and Mars mobility, and future off-world movement systems;
- unknown or unclassified moving entities represented through verified observations and capabilities without assuming an unsupported identity or origin.

Earth is an operating environment, not the architectural boundary.

## Movement Resolution Loop

1. **Need** — what must happen?
2. **Want** — what does the participant prefer?
3. **Subject** — what or who is moving?
4. **Purpose** — why is it moving?
5. **Capacity** — how much can be moved?
6. **Capability** — what can perform or support the movement?
7. **Resource** — what is available?
8. **Route/Trajectory** — where can it move?
9. **Environment** — what physical environment governs movement?
10. **Context** — what surrounds the movement?
11. **Signals** — what is changing now or expected to change?
12. **Services** — what support is required or desired?
13. **Rules** — what permissions, constraints and obligations apply?
14. **Time** — when must movement occur?
15. **Curation** — which viable option best satisfies the objective?
16. **Execution** — allocate and perform the movement.
17. **Guidance** — continuously advise and coordinate.
18. **Adaptation** — respond to changing conditions.
19. **Completion** — establish the movement outcome.
20. **Pulse** — feed verified outcomes and feedback back into the ecosystem.

## Capability and Capacity

A vehicle is not the primary abstraction. A vehicle is a resource that exposes capabilities and capacity.

Examples include:

- passenger seats;
- cargo weight and volume;
- luggage capacity;
- accessibility and mobility support;
- towing/recovery capacity;
- medical capability and equipment;
- refrigeration or temperature control;
- animal transport capability;
- off-road or specialised terrain capability;
- range, fuel, charging and energy capability;
- driver/operator qualifications;
- loading, unloading, guiding, escorting or other service capability.

Matching is therefore based on **need-to-capability fit**, not simply availability.

## Movement Chains

A movement may contain any number of compatible modes and services:

`walk -> Along -> bus -> taxi -> walk`

`farm -> local vehicle -> truck -> warehouse -> delivery vehicle -> recipient`

`airport -> shuttle -> hotel -> event transport -> venue -> hotel -> airport`

`vehicle failure -> recovery -> workshop -> replacement vehicle -> destination`

`Earth -> launch -> orbit -> transfer -> lunar orbit -> lunar surface`

The system composes these as one movement where appropriate rather than forcing each leg into an isolated product.

## Context and Signals

The movement graph may include roads, routes, junctions, bus stops, landmarks, villages, toll gates, checkpoints, lawfully available enforcement information, traffic, construction, weather, flooding, bridges, crossings, charging, fuel, service facilities, hospitals, accommodation, markets, events, connectivity and other relevant infrastructure or conditions.

Signals may originate from authoritative infrastructure, participating vehicles, sensors, service providers, communities, historical observations and other permitted sources. Provenance, freshness and confidence must remain explicit.

## Journey Services

Services are composable capabilities attached to movement, including:

- insurance and protection products;
- roadside assistance and recovery;
- charging, power outlets and power banks;
- connectivity and communication support;
- audio, entertainment and journey content;
- food, water and comfort services;
- medical and accessibility support;
- drivers, guides, operators and loading crews;
- maintenance, cleaning and vehicle services;
- storage and accommodation;
- event, holiday, tourism, cultural and destination services.

## Product Composition

Universal Movement is the substrate. Products are configurations or interfaces over it, including but not limited to:

- Charter — organised and coordinated movement;
- Rental — temporary access to vehicles or movement capacity;
- Along — participating commercial shared movement using existing corridor capacity;
- Hitch — conditional social/adventure movement where commercial transport is not the defining purpose;
- Logistico — goods and freight movement;
- Delivery — local and last-mile fulfilment;
- Pilgrim — purpose-driven, multi-stage journeys;
- event movement;
- travel and holiday journeys;
- fleet and operator management;
- emergency and specialised movement;
- future autonomous and robotic movement.

New products must reuse the Universal Movement substrate rather than creating parallel movement primitives.

## Participation and Governance

Commercial, social, institutional and public movement may have different eligibility, permissions, economic and governance rules. The system must distinguish them rather than collapsing all movement into one commercial model.

In particular, Along must enable legitimate participation in existing transport ecosystems rather than indiscriminately bypassing established operators, queues, associations or regulatory structures.

Hitch remains conceptually distinct from commercial shared transport: social participation and shared experience are primary, with applicable cost-sharing and safety constraints handled explicitly.

## Offline and Resilient Movement

Movement must remain useful across connectivity gaps. A movement package may contain locally available routes, landmarks, planned stops, emergency information, constraints, alternatives and other required information for offline execution and later synchronisation.

## Future-Proofing

The architecture must not encode current vehicle forms, road networks or terrestrial geography as permanent assumptions. It may encode verified physical, safety, legal and environmental constraints while remaining extensible to new modes and environments.

Future systems may therefore introduce new vehicles, propulsion systems, autonomous agents, orbital infrastructure, lunar/Mars infrastructure or other movement technologies without requiring a new foundational movement model.

Unknown or unclassified entities are represented through evidence, observations and known/unknown capabilities; the system must not convert uncertainty into unsupported factual claims.

## Architectural Lock

**Universal Movement is the permanent abstraction.**

Transport is an instance of movement.

Charter is an orchestration product over Universal Movement.

Along, Hitch, Rental, Logistico, Delivery, Pilgrim, event movement, travel and future products are composed from the same substrate.

The system is designed to accommodate the next movement category rather than requiring the architecture to be redesigned when one appears.
