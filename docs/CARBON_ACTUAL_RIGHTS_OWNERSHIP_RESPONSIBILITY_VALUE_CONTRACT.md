# Carbon Actual Rights, Ownership, Responsibility & Value Contract

**Status:** Canonical kernel contract

Carbon Actual must distinguish rights, ownership, custody, stewardship, responsibility, obligation, liability and value. They are related but are not interchangeable.

## 1. Rights

A Right is an authorized claim or permission concerning an object, resource, capability, relationship, action or outcome.

Rights may include:

- access
- use
- control
- modify
- transfer
- license
- benefit
- exclude
- participate
- receive
- delegate
- revoke

A right is scoped by subject, object, action, context, time and authority.

## 2. Ownership

Ownership is a governed bundle of rights and responsibilities. It is not synonymous with possession, custody or control.

Ownership may be:

- individual
- joint
- organizational
- collective
- fractional
- delegated
- conditional
- time-bounded
- governed by contract or law

## 3. Custody

Custody means currently holding, operating, safeguarding or managing something. Custody does not automatically imply ownership.

## 4. Stewardship

Stewardship means responsibility for maintaining or advancing the condition, purpose or continuity of something. A steward may have no ownership right.

## 5. Responsibility

Responsibility identifies the party accountable for an action, condition, outcome or obligation.

Responsibility may arise from:

- authority
- contract
- role
- law
- delegation
- accepted commitment
- causation

## 6. Obligation

An obligation is a binding requirement to perform, provide, refrain, maintain, repay, compensate or otherwise satisfy an agreed or authoritative condition.

## 7. Liability

Liability represents an attributable burden, obligation, exposure or negative responsibility recognized by the applicable economic/governance model.

Liability must identify its basis, subject, amount or measure, conditions, due state and settlement path.

## 8. Asset

An asset represents recognized positive economic/resource capacity or beneficial claim under the applicable valuation model.

An object can simultaneously have different asset/liability positions for different subjects or contexts.

## 9. Carbon Actual inverted economics

Carbon Actual may classify outcomes using an inverted economic relationship between contribution and feedback.

Let:

- `S` = recognized value sent/contributed by an actor/system
- `P` = recognized Pulse/value returned or produced as feedback
- `Δ = P - S`

The canonical kernel does **not** hard-code one universal monetary interpretation of `Δ`.

Instead, the domain economic policy interprets the sign and magnitude:

- positive `Δ` may represent a liability-bearing surplus/obligation where the ecosystem has produced more recognized value relative to the contribution;
- negative `Δ` may represent an asset-bearing claim or deficit requiring care/recovery under the applicable policy;
- near-zero `Δ` represents the equilibrium/median region.

The exact classification must be policy-versioned, auditable and context-aware.

This preserves the user's inverted-economic doctrine without confusing mathematical difference with legal or financial liability.

## 10. Value is multidimensional

Value is not restricted to money.

A value assessment may include:

- time
- energy
- labor/effort
- attention
- data contribution
- compute
- resource consumption
- distance/travel
- wear/depreciation
- opportunity cost
- risk
- knowledge
- capability
- social contribution
- environmental/resource impact
- financial value

The valuation engine must preserve component measurements so a composite value can be explained and audited.

## 11. Value layers

```text
OBSERVATION
   ↓
MEASUREMENT
   ↓
NORMALIZATION
   ↓
VALUATION POLICY
   ↓
VALUE
   ↓
ECONOMIC CLASSIFICATION
   ↓

```

A raw measurement must not silently become a monetary value.

## 12. Median/equilibrium value

Carbon Actual may maintain context-specific reference values representing a sustainable or expected baseline.

A reference baseline is not an absolute universal number. It is derived from policy, context, population/resource conditions and time.

Therefore:

`equilibrium = context-specific reference state`

not merely a fixed token price.

## 13. Fractionalization and decimalization

Rights and value may be represented fractionally or with arbitrary precision where governance and technical limits permit.

Fractionalization changes the unit of participation/ownership.

Decimalization changes representational granularity.

They are related but distinct.

## 14. Tokenization

Tokenization represents a defined right, claim, resource, value unit, credential, access condition or other canonical object in a transferable or programmable representation.

A token is not automatically the underlying asset or right. The legal/economic relationship must be explicit.

## 15. Democratization

Democratization concerns distribution of access, participation, ownership, decision rights or opportunity according to the applicable governance policy.

It must not be confused with indiscriminate access.

## 16. Decentralization

Decentralization concerns distribution of control, verification, infrastructure or decision authority.

A decentralized technical mechanism does not automatically make a governance system decentralized.

## 17. Settlement

Settlement resolves an obligation, claim, allocation or economic event.

Settlement may involve:

- transfer
- compensation
- credit/debit
- service completion
- resource return
- maintenance
- remediation
- cancellation
- forgiveness
- restructuring

## 19. No hidden liability

A user, organization or intelligence must not silently acquire an economic or governance liability merely because the system inferred an action or recommendation.

Liability requires a recognized basis under the applicable policy, contract, authority or law.

## 20. No waste principle

Carbon Actual treats residual value as potentially recoverable information, material, capability, energy, learning, resource or feedback.

Unusable residue can be classified as Ash according to the Ash/security policy rather than silently disappearing.

## 21. Rights and privacy

Rights over data must be represented separately from access permissions. A system may process information under delegated authority without acquiring ownership of the underlying person's identity or interests.

## 22. Universal rule

Product branches must reuse this model for ownership, workforce, contracts, investments, education, property, transport, maintenance, government services, marketplaces and other domains rather than inventing separate economic grammars.
