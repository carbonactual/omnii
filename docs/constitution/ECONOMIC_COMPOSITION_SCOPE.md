# OMNII ECONOMIC COMPOSITION — FULL SCOPE

**Status: CANONICAL SCOPE / HARDENING CONTRACT**  
**Scope:** fractionalization, decimalization, democratization, tokenization, minting, settlement, distributed ledgers, blockchain, value vectors, ecosystem economics, and all participating entities.

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the complete economic-composition capability of OMNII. It is deliberately broader than cryptocurrency. The system must be able to represent real-world and digital resources, capabilities, rights, obligations, access, use, ownership, value, feedback, representations, issuance, settlement and shared verification without making any one implementation the constitutional substrate.

The design is open-world: new economic objects, dimensions, rights, mechanisms, technologies and settlement rails can be added without changing the meaning of existing primitives.

## 2. Foundational Rule

**Every meaningful function transforms one or more resources, capabilities, states, rights, obligations or forms of value.**

The ecosystem therefore records:

```text
INPUT / GIVEN
→ TRANSFORMATION
→ USE / CONSUMPTION
→ OUTPUT / ENABLEMENT
→ OUTCOME
→ PULSE / RETURN
→ RECONCILIATION
→ LEARNING / CONTINUITY
```

Money is one settlement/valuation representation, not the definition of value.

## 3. Canonical Definitions

### Value
A contextual, evidenced assessment of contribution, consumption, preservation, enablement, outcome, opportunity, risk or other economically meaningful effect.

### Value Vector
A multidimensional representation of value. Dimensions remain independently measurable and explainable before any aggregate is calculated.

### Resource
Anything that can be consumed, transformed, preserved, allocated, transferred, degraded, replenished or otherwise participate in a function.

Examples: time, energy, water, materials, attention, data, connectivity, compute, money, land, capacity, infrastructure, knowledge, human effort and environmental capacity.

### Capability
The ability available to perform a function or produce an outcome. A capability may be owned, shared, rented, delegated, accessed, fractionalized or composed from other capabilities.

### Function
A bounded activity or transformation that consumes, commits or mobilizes resources/capabilities and produces an outcome or state change.

### Fractionalization
Allocation or use of a defined fraction of a larger resource, capacity, right, capability, ownership interest, access space or participation space.

Fractionalization answers: **how much of the larger thing is actually involved?**

### Decimalization
Representation of quantity, intensity, quality, level or resolution at a domain-supported granularity.

Decimalization answers: **how precisely or at what level is the selected thing represented?** It must not imply precision that the measurement system cannot support.

### Democratization
A governance and access mechanism that makes capability, opportunity or participation available to a broader population subject to legitimate constraints, scarcity, safety, authority, consent and fairness rules.

Democratization does not mean equal ownership, equal consumption or identical outcomes.

### Entitlement
A governed right to access, receive, use, consume, participate in, control, claim or benefit from something under stated conditions.

### Tokenization
Creation of a digital representation that refers to a governed underlying object, state, right, claim, allocation, receipt, credential, entitlement or value exposure.

A token is a representation; the underlying semantics remain defined by the referenced object, authority, contract, jurisdiction and provenance.

### Token
An addressable digital representation of a governed underlying object or relationship. Tokens may be fungible, non-fungible, semi-fungible, composable, consumable, redeemable, revocable, transferable, non-transferable or otherwise constrained.

### Minting
Authorized issuance of a token or representation according to an issuance policy. Minting creates representation/supply; it does not automatically create underlying value.

### Burning / Retirement
Controlled removal, retirement, cancellation or invalidation of a token representation according to its lifecycle rules. Burning does not automatically destroy the underlying real-world object or right.

### Settlement
The process by which obligations, transfers, claims or entitlements are finalized or recorded between parties or systems.

### Ledger
A record of economic events, balances, obligations, ownership/custody states, allocations or settlements. A ledger may be centralized, distributed, append-only, cryptographically committed or hybrid.

### Blockchain / DLT
A class of distributed-ledger infrastructure providing shared state, ordering, cryptographic commitments and programmable rules. It is an implementation option, not a prerequisite for OMNII tokenization or economic accounting.

### Provenance
The trace of origin, transformation, custody, authority, evidence and history associated with an object, claim, value or representation.

### Custody
Control or possession without necessarily implying ownership.

### Ownership
A governed relationship defining legally or constitutionally recognized rights and interests. Ownership is never inferred merely from possession of a token unless the governing instrument explicitly makes that connection.

### Authority
The legitimate power to create, approve, transfer, revoke, mint, burn, allocate, settle or otherwise act on an object or relationship.

### Issuer
An entity or authorized system that creates a representation or credential under defined authority.

### Holder
An entity that possesses or controls a representation or credential. Holder status does not by itself establish ownership.

### Custodian
An entity responsible for holding, safeguarding or administering an object or representation for itself or another party.

### Verifier
An entity or process that checks claims, representations, evidence, signatures, status, provenance or policy conformance.

### Redemption
Exchange of a token/representation for the underlying entitlement, resource, service, claim or settlement defined by its governing rules.

### Transfer
A change in control, custody, allocation, entitlement, ownership or representation according to applicable rules. Transferability must be explicitly defined.

## 7. Inverted Asset / Liability Economics

For a defined subject, function, period and context:

```text
Pulse Returned > Value Given → LIABILITY
Pulse Returned < Value Given → ASSET
Pulse Returned = Value Given → BALANCED
```

The classification is a dynamic ecosystem state under the OMNII inverted-economic doctrine. It is not a replacement for statutory financial reporting definitions, and conventional accounting views may coexist as separate interpretations.

Magnitude must be preserved independently from classification.

## 8. Ecosystem Value Architecture

Value vectors aggregate without destroying their sources:

```text
Individual
→ Group
→ Community
→ Organization
→ Territory
→ Network
→ Ecosystem
```

The ecosystem maintains at least three distinct views:

### General Ecosystem Vector
Aggregate measured value/capability across the defined ecosystem scope.

### Average
Contextual benchmark of available or realized capability/value across the selected population, capacity base or period.

### Floor
Protected minimum capability or value condition defined by governance and context.

### Safe Haven
Protected reserve capacity maintained for resilience, continuity, emergencies and future uncertainty. It must not be treated as ordinary surplus merely because it exists.

The system must distinguish historical aggregate value, current capacity, committed capacity, available capacity, latent capacity and protected reserve.

## 18. Credentials and Claims

Economic and entitlement systems may depend on credentials. OMNII should support issuer/holder/verifier roles, claims, validity periods, evidence, status, selective disclosure and machine-verifiable proofs.

W3C Verifiable Credentials provide a useful interoperability model: credentials are sets of claims with issuer/holder/verifier roles, and current W3C work includes status, evidence, authorization, time, privacy and zero-knowledge proof extension points. OMNII should map to these concepts without making W3C credentials the constitutional identity model.

## 23. Standards and External Reference Map

The implementation should remain standards-aware and map rather than blindly copy external standards.

Relevant references include:

- **ISO 55000:2024 / ISO 55001:2024** for lifecycle asset management, realization of value, risk, performance and asset-management systems.
- **ISO 24165-1:2025 / ISO 24165-2:2025** for digital token identifiers and registry data elements.
- **ISO 23257:2022** for blockchain/DLT reference architecture; ISO/AWI 23257 is the current revision work item.
- **ISO/AWI 26251** for tokenization methods in DLTs, including governance and deployment-context considerations.
- **ISO/TR 6039:2023** for identifiers of subjects and objects relevant to blockchain interoperability.
- **W3C Verifiable Credentials Data Model** for claims, issuer/holder/verifier roles, presentations, evidence, status, authorization and privacy-preserving verification.
- **Ethereum ERC-20** for fungible token interface patterns.
- **ERC-721** for unique/non-fungible token patterns.
- **ERC-1155** for multi-token and semi-fungible patterns.
- **ISO 31000** and related risk-management guidance for uncertainty/risk treatment.
- **ISO 22301** for continuity/resilience.
- **ISO 37301** for compliance management.

These are interoperability and reference layers. OMNII remains constitution-first.

## 26. Open-World Rule

If a future object appears—new technology, new economic instrument, new entity, unknown phenomenon, extraterrestrial artifact, novel digital organism, new settlement mechanism, or unknown form of value—the system must be able to register it provisionally and describe its observed properties, claims, relationships, capabilities, resource participation, value dimensions and governance without forcing it into an inappropriate existing class.

## 27. Required Invariants

1. No token exists without an issuance context.
2. No issuance exists without authority.
3. No token representation silently changes the meaning of its underlying.
4. No fraction exists without a defined reference basis.
5. No decimal precision implies unsupported measurement accuracy.
6. No democratization rule bypasses safety, authority, consent or lawful constraints.
7. No blockchain record is treated as proof that an off-chain event actually occurred without appropriate evidence.
8. No aggregate value may erase its dimensional components.
9. No asset/liability classification may erase the underlying vector or context.
10. No burn/retirement operation silently destroys the underlying real-world object.
11. No transfer silently changes ownership when it only changes custody or entitlement.
12. No economic event is complete until expected and actual states can be reconciled or an explicit exception remains open.
13. Historical states remain interpretable after schema or policy evolution.
14. Unknowns remain unknown until evidence supports a stronger classification.
15. Every new economic mechanism must plug into the existing identity, state, time, causality, epistemic, governance, execution, reconciliation and continuity layers.

## 28. Design Objective

The goal is not to build a cryptocurrency system.

The goal is to build a **general economic composition system for reality and digital reality** in which resources, capabilities, value, access, rights, obligations and representations can be measured, composed, fractionalized, decimalized, democratized, tokenized, issued, transferred, settled, reconciled and continuously evaluated without losing provenance or constitutional coherence.

Products are compositions. Tokens are representations. Ledgers are records. Blockchain is optional infrastructure. Money is one valuation/settlement mechanism. **The ecosystem remains the substrate.**
