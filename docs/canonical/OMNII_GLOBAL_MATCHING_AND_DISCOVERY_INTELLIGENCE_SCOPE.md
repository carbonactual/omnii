# OMNII — Global Matching & Discovery Intelligence Capability

**Status:** CANONICAL CAPABILITY SCOPE  
**Scope:** Global-by-default  
**Layer:** Shared intelligence capability connecting Sourcing, Opportunity and ecosystem participants

## 1. Why this capability exists

Sourcing and Opportunity are distinct:

- **Sourcing** finds ways to satisfy an existing requirement.
- **Opportunity** finds potential value or possibility that could be unlocked.

Both require a third capability to determine what relates to what.

**Global Matching & Discovery Intelligence** is that connective layer.

It answers:

> **“Given these needs, capabilities, people, assets, conditions and constraints, what meaningful relationships or possibilities exist?”**

It is the ecosystem's reusable discovery, matching, recommendation, ranking and relationship-intelligence layer.

## 2. Global-by-default

It may search and reason across:

```text

```

## 4. Core functions

### Discovery
Find relevant entities, records, signals, capabilities and relationships.

### Matching
Determine whether two or more objects can satisfy one another's requirements.

### Ranking
Order possible matches according to declared objectives and evidence.

### Recommendation
Explain which paths appear strongest and why.

### Graph traversal
Discover non-obvious relationships beyond keyword search.

### Composition
Combine multiple matches into one executable pathway.

### Gap detection
Identify missing links required to turn a match into action.

### Re-discovery
Continuously search for better alternatives as conditions change.

## 7. Exact and approximate matching

The engine supports:

- exact match
- compatible match
- equivalent match
- substitute match
- partial match
- complementary match
- sequence match
- multi-party match
- probabilistic candidate match

Approximate matches must expose material differences and confidence.

## 8. Multi-party matching

Many real-world needs cannot be satisfied by two entities.

The engine can compose:

```text
buyer
+ manufacturer
+ inspector
+ financier
+ insurer
+ logistics provider
```

or:

```text
farmer group
+ processor
+ warehouse
+ exporter
+ buyer
+ finance
```

The match object preserves which participant satisfies which requirement.

## 9. Recommendation engine

Recommendations should answer:

```text
WHAT
WHY
FOR WHOM
FROM WHERE
TO WHERE
AT WHAT COST/VALUE
BY WHEN
WITH WHAT RISK
WHAT EVIDENCE SUPPORTS IT
WHAT IS UNKNOWN
WHAT MUST HAPPEN NEXT
```

The engine should normally surface a small set of materially different pathways rather than bury the user under unranked candidates.

## 10. Explainability

Every meaningful recommendation should be explainable through:

- requirement fit
- evidence
- relevant constraints
- economic/value factors
- timing
- risk
- performance history
- alternatives considered
- unresolved uncertainty

The user must be able to understand why a match or recommendation appeared.

## 11. Evidence hierarchy

Discovery should distinguish:

```text
known
verified
reported
estimated
inferred
AI-generated hypothesis
unverified
outdated
unavailable
```

A lower-confidence candidate may still be surfaced, but its status must be visible.

## 12. Confidence and freshness

Every dynamic recommendation should have:

- evidence timestamp
- freshness window
- source provenance
- confidence
- change history

Stale supply/capacity/price/availability should not appear equivalent to live evidence.

## 13. Semantic rather than keyword discovery

The system should understand that:

> “I need a machine to make this”

may imply the need for:

- equipment
- power
- tooling
- operator skill
- facility
- maintenance
- financing
- installation
- training

Similarly:

> “I want to sell what I produce.”

may imply:

- buyer discovery
- standards
- packaging
- export readiness
- logistics
- payment
- financing
- market entry.

## 15. Global market comparison

Where legally and technically permitted, matching may compare geographically distributed paths:

```text
local
vs regional
vs national
vs continental
vs global
```

It should account for total economics and feasibility rather than headline price alone.

## 16. Market and capacity inference

The engine may infer:

- emerging demand
- unmet demand
- likely supplier gaps
- spare capacity
- complementary capabilities
- geographic imbalance
- seasonality
- substitution possibilities
- emerging markets

Inferred intelligence must remain labelled as inference and must not be represented as confirmed fact.

## 17. Negative matching

A good discovery system must know when not to match.

Reasons may include:

- incompatible specification
- insufficient capacity
- expired certification
- regulatory disqualification
- impossible timing
- unacceptable risk
- poor performance evidence
- economics do not work
- missing required capability

Negative results become reusable intelligence and may inform future opportunity creation.

## 18. Match failure and remediation

When a match cannot execute, the engine can identify missing capability:

```text
match
→ missing inspection
→ source inspector

match
→ missing finance
→ source finance

match
→ missing machine
→ source machinery

match
→ missing certification
→ source testing/certification path
```

This allows a failed direct match to become a composed match.

## 19. Aggregated matching

The engine can match at different scales:

```text
individual ↔ individual
individual ↔ business
business ↔ business
business ↔ institution
group ↔ supplier
network ↔ network
country/region demand ↔ global supply
```

It may aggregate compatible requirements before matching to improve economic feasibility.

## 20. Reverse discovery

For every significant capability or asset, the engine may search for:

- potential customers
- complementary providers
- partners
- funding
- underused capacity utilization
- markets
- export destinations
- substitute users

Thus a capability can discover its own demand.

## 21. Temporal intelligence

Matches can be time-sensitive.

The engine must represent:

- now
- scheduled
- recurring
- seasonal
- expiring
- future capacity
- temporary surplus
- limited-time opportunity

A match that is valid tomorrow may not be valid next month.

## 22. Relationship intelligence

The engine may consider authorized relationship data such as:

- prior transactions
- previous performance
- existing contracts
- participant preferences
- trusted networks
- geographic familiarity
- supplier history

Relationship information must respect privacy, access control and purpose limitation.

## 23. Fraud, manipulation and adversarial resilience

Discovery must be resilient to:

- duplicate listings
- synthetic demand
- fake capacity
- manipulated prices
- identity fraud
- review manipulation
- coordinated spam
- stale records
- deceptive specifications

Signals may be downgraded, quarantined or routed for verification without silently deleting evidence.

## 24. Discovery channels

- ecosystem registries

- marketplace data
- procurement systems
- public records
- trade sources
- institutional feeds
- partner networks

## 25. Opportunity creation interface

When matching reveals a relationship that does not yet exist, the engine may create an Opportunity candidate:

```text
MATCH FOUND
→ potential value identified
→ opportunity candidate
→ qualification
```

This is a boundary between Matching Intelligence and Global Opportunity.

## 26. Sourcing interface

When matching identifies a provider or source capable of satisfying a concrete requirement, the result may enter Global Sourcing:

```text
REQUIREMENT
→ MATCH
→ SOURCE CANDIDATE
→ SOURCING QUALIFICATION
```

This prevents the matching layer from becoming a duplicate supplier registry.

## 28. Optimization objectives

The engine can optimize for declared objectives such as:

- lowest acceptable total cost
- fastest feasible path
- highest confidence
- highest quality
- lowest risk
- local-first
- regional-first
- global best fit
- maximum resilience
- maximum capacity utilization
- maximum measured value
- sustainability objectives where defined
- participant-specific priorities

No single objective is constitutionally universal.

## 29. Human authority

Discovery and ranking may be automated within defined permissions.

Material commitments, regulated actions, financial commitments, legal acceptance and consequential decisions remain subject to the appropriate authority and confirmation model.

ABBA can orchestrate and explain but cannot manufacture human consent.

## 32. Non-goals

This capability does not become:

- the marketplace
- the sourcing registry
- the opportunity registry
- the trading system
- the regulator
- the financial institution
- the insurer
- the identity authority

It is the ecosystem's **relationship and recommendation intelligence layer**.

## 33. Canonical invariant

> **Matching & Discovery Intelligence connects requirements, supply, capabilities, assets, people, markets and conditions. It enables Sourcing and Opportunity without replacing either.**
