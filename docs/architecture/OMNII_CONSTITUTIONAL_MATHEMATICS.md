# OMNII Constitutional Mathematics v1.0

**Status: Constitutional Doctrine — Foundational mathematical semantics**

**Canonical relationship:** This document extends the OMNII Constitutional Architecture and OMNII Constitutional Semantics. It does not create a new architecture, phase, runtime, authority, or product model.

**Purpose:** Define the mathematical behavior of participation, value, contribution, return, trust, relationship, Pulse, resources, scarcity, abundance, stewardship, obligation, reciprocity, reputation and continuity across the OMNII ecosystem.

OMNII Constitutional Mathematics is not accounting, bookkeeping, finance, or a single monetary formula. It is a formal language for reasoning about change, contribution, consequence, exchange, responsibility, confidence and continuity across physical, digital, biological, social and institutional reality.

---

## 0. Constitutional Scope

OMNII already establishes that:

- participation is universal without implying equivalence;
- identity does not imply authority;
- capability does not imply permission;
- intelligence does not imply legitimacy;
- ownership does not imply stewardship;
- representation is not the thing represented;
- connectivity does not imply consent;
- autonomy does not imply sovereignty;
- consequential activity produces state and evidence;
- Pulse describes consequential movement/change;
- Becoming records transformation through movement, evidence and learning.

Mathematics must preserve these distinctions rather than collapse them into a single score.

The central constitutional object is therefore not a number but a **state relation**:

`participant + context + relationship + authority + capability + resource + action + consequence + evidence + time → next state`

Numerical representations are permitted only where the relevant quantity, unit, uncertainty, authority and measurement context are explicit.

# 1. First Principles

### 1.1 State before score

Every material mathematical claim refers to a defined state, time interval and context.

`X(t, c)` = state of object or relationship `X` at time `t` in context `c`.

A number without its semantic context is not a complete constitutional measurement.

### 1.2 Difference before conclusion

Change is primary:

`ΔX = X(t₂) − X(t₁)`

Where a quantity is non-additive, its change must use the appropriate domain operation rather than forced arithmetic subtraction.

### 1.3 Contribution before return

Return is interpretable only relative to what was contributed, enabled, consumed, preserved or obligated.

`Contribution ≠ Return`

and neither is automatically equal to price, money, ownership or profit.

### 1.4 Relationship is part of the quantity

A contribution or return without its relationship context may be materially misleading. Where the relationship changes, the interpretation of the quantity may change even if the numeric amount does not.

### 1.5 Evidence bounds inference

Observed, verified, inferred, predicted, simulated, reported, claimed, disputed and unknown values must remain epistemically distinguishable.

### 1.6 Time matters

Value, trust, reputation, resource availability and obligation are dynamic.

`Q(t)` is not equivalent to a timeless `Q`.

### 1.7 Boundaries matter

Measurements are valid only within their defined scope, authority, jurisdiction, unit and observation window.

### 1.8 No universal scalar for reality

The ecosystem must not reduce human worth, rights, legitimacy, consent, identity or constitutional authority to one score.

### 1.9 Mathematics cannot manufacture authority

A mathematically optimal action remains unauthorized when authority is absent.

`Optimization ≠ Legitimacy`

### 1.10 Every consequential mathematical model must be auditable

A material calculation SHOULD preserve:

`inputs + units + source + method + assumptions + uncertainty + timestamp + authority + output`

# 2. Value Doctrine

Value is the measured significance of a change, condition, capability, resource, service, relationship, outcome or preserved possibility within a defined context.

Value may be monetary or non-monetary and may include, where legitimately measurable:

- time;
- human effort;
- intelligence or compute;
- energy;
- materials;
- distance and mobility;
- data and knowledge;
- attention;
- capital;
- risk carried or reduced;
- access enabled;
- capability created;
- maintenance performed;
- harm prevented;
- useful output produced;
- continuity preserved;
- opportunity enabled.

A value statement therefore takes the form:

`V = M(q, u, s, t, c, e)`

where:

- `q` = quantity;
- `u` = unit or measurement basis;
- `s` = source;
- `t` = time;
- `c` = context;
- `e` = evidence/epistemic status;
- `M` = the applicable measurement method.

Value is not identical to price.

Value is not identical to money.

Value is not identical to ownership.

Value is not identical to usefulness in every context.

The same resource can possess different recognized values under different legitimate contexts without changing the identity of the underlying thing.

# 3. Return Doctrine

Return is what comes back to a participant, relationship, community, institution, ecosystem or future state as a consequence of contribution, provision, stewardship, investment, service, preservation or other authorized participation.

Return may be:

`direct | indirect | delayed | distributed | non-monetary | avoided-harm | capability | continuity | knowledge | relationship | residual`

A return may be realized by a different participant from the original contributor. Therefore:

`Contributor ≠ Recipient`

unless the relevant relationship explicitly makes them the same.

### Return relation

For a defined observation window:

`R_w = recognized return within window w`

`C_w = recognized contribution within window w`

A return relationship may be analyzed through:

`D_w = R_w − C_w`

where `D_w` is a **value differential**, not an automatic accounting profit or loss.

Interpretation requires context, obligations, risk, quality, time, uncertainty and jurisdiction.

A positive differential does not prove fairness.

A negative differential does not prove failure.

A zero differential does not prove equilibrium.

---

# 4. Contribution Doctrine

Contribution is the measured effect of what a participant provides, enables, preserves, absorbs, coordinates, repairs, teaches, protects or makes possible for another participant or the wider ecosystem.

Contribution can occur through:

`work + capital + intelligence + care + stewardship + infrastructure + knowledge + coordination + risk absorption + maintenance + opportunity creation`

The mathematical contribution of an act should be evaluated over the relevant time horizon rather than only at the instant of execution.

For a contribution sequence:

`C_total = Σ C_i`

provided the components are commensurable. When they are not, OMNII must preserve a vector or structured representation rather than falsely collapsing them into one unit.

### Contribution integrity

A contribution claim should preserve:

`actor + object + relation + action + resource + context + time + evidence + recognized effect`

Contribution is not authority.

Contribution is not ownership.

Contribution is not entitlement unless a valid contract, policy, law or constitutional relation creates that entitlement.

---

# 5. Trust Mathematics

Trust is a modeled expectation of reliable behavior, evidence quality, integrity or fulfillment under specified conditions.

Trust is not identity, consent, authority or truth.

Let:

`T(x, y, c, t) ∈ [0,1]`

represent contextual trust of `x` toward `y` under context `c` at time `t`, where a bounded score is appropriate.

Trust SHOULD be decomposable into evidence-bearing dimensions such as:

`reliability + integrity + competence + consistency + transparency + recourse`

rather than treated as a universal scalar.

A contextual trust update may be represented abstractly as:

`T_next = F(T_prior, E_pos, E_neg, recency, relevance, independence, severity, recovery)`

where `F` is a governed model rather than a universal frozen formula.

### Trust invariants

1. Trust decays when evidence becomes stale where recency matters.
2. Repeated reliable behavior may increase confidence.
3. One severe event may carry disproportionate weight relative to many minor events.
4. Recovery behavior may restore some trust without erasing history.
5. Trust must retain provenance.
6. Trust must remain contextual; trust in one role does not automatically transfer to another.
7. Trust does not grant authority.

---

# 6. Relationship Mathematics

A relationship is a typed, contextual and time-bounded relation between participants.

`Rel(x, y, r, t, c)`

where `r` identifies the relation type and `c` its context.

Examples include:

`owns | stewards | governs | authorizes | depends-on | provides | consumes | represents | observes | actuates | communicates-with | cares-for | employs | serves | located-in | part-of`

Relationships may carry:

`strength + direction + reciprocity + obligations + rights + permissions + evidence + temporal validity`

### Relationship change

`ΔRel = Rel(t₂) ⊖ Rel(t₁)`

The operator `⊖` is domain-specific because relationships are not ordinary scalar quantities.

Relationship mathematics must preserve distinctions between:

`ownership ≠ custody ≠ stewardship ≠ guardianship ≠ responsibility ≠ jurisdiction ≠ authority`

### Relationship value

A relationship may create, destroy, preserve or redistribute value without itself being reducible to value.

`Relationship effect → Value consequence`

but not:

`Relationship = Value`

# 7. Pulse Mathematics

Pulse is the ecosystem abstraction for consequential movement and feedback.

For an observation window:

`P_w = Σ p_i`

where each `p_i` is a governed Pulse event or measured contribution to Pulse.

Pulse may encode:

- activity;
- movement;
- interaction;
- transaction;
- observation;
- state transition;
- communication;
- risk;
- resource consumption;
- resource creation;
- proof;
- failure or recovery.

Pulse is not currency.

Pulse is not permission.

Pulse is not authority.

Pulse is not automatically Value.

Instead:

`Pulse → Evaluation → possible Value / Trust / Learning / Becoming consequences`

The sensitivity of a Pulse metric MUST be proportionate to its purpose. High-volume low-significance activity must not automatically outweigh low-volume high-significance events.

---

# 8. Resource Mathematics

Resources are bounded or unbounded capacities, materials, permissions, capabilities, information, time, energy or other means required for activity.

A resource state may be represented as:

`Res(t) = available + committed + reserved + consumed + recoverable + degraded`

subject to domain-specific conservation and transformation rules.

### Resource flow

`Input → allocation → use → transformation → residual → recovery / disposal`

For a resource class with conservation semantics:

`available_t + inflow_t − committed_t − consumed_t + recovered_t = available_{t+1}`

where each term must be measured in compatible units.

Not all resources obey simple conservation. Knowledge, trust, reputation and capability may be created, transformed or degraded according to different rules.

### Resource integrity

Resource mathematics must distinguish:

`physical quantity + quality + availability + accessibility + authority + location + time`

A resource that exists physically but cannot be lawfully or technically accessed is not equivalent to fully available resource capacity.

# 9. Scarcity Doctrine

Scarcity is a condition in which desired claims on a resource, capacity, opportunity or attention exceed the relevant available capacity under defined constraints.

`Scarcity(x,c,t) = Demand(x,c,t) > Available(x,c,t)`

Scarcity is contextual rather than absolute.

A scarce resource in one territory may be abundant in another.

A scarce resource now may be abundant later.

A resource may be physically abundant while access, trust, infrastructure or authority is scarce.

Scarcity analysis must therefore distinguish:

`physical scarcity | access scarcity | capability scarcity | temporal scarcity | information scarcity | trust scarcity | authority scarcity`

Scarcity does not automatically justify exclusion, extraction or price maximization.

Where appropriate, the ecosystem should prefer:

`reduce demand + increase capacity + recover residuals + improve coordination + substitute + share + preserve reserves`

before treating scarcity as an excuse for unnecessary extraction.

---

# 10. Abundance Doctrine

Abundance is a condition in which relevant available capacity materially exceeds immediate legitimate demand within a defined context.

`Abundance(x,c,t) = Available(x,c,t) > Demand(x,c,t)`

Abundance is not waste.

Unused capacity may become:

`reserve | maintenance capacity | shared capacity | opportunity | investment | recovery feedstock`

The constitutional objective is not maximal consumption but **useful circulation and continuity**.

A system approaching abundance should increase access, resilience, recovery and capability rather than manufacture artificial scarcity.

Abundance can be fragile. Surplus exists only under the conditions that sustain it.

---

# 11. Stewardship Mathematics

Stewardship measures the responsibility to preserve, maintain, improve, transfer or responsibly conclude the condition of an object, resource, relationship, institution, territory or ecosystem.

Let:

`S(x,o,c,t)` = stewardship state of participant `x` over object/resource `o` in context `c` at time `t`.

Stewardship can include:

`care + maintenance + protection + renewal + monitoring + repair + handover + accountability`

A stewardship obligation may be represented as:

`S_obligation = expected care − fulfilled care`

The expression is conceptual and only collapses to a scalar when the care dimensions are commensurable.

### Stewardship invariant

The party with the greatest control or benefit is not automatically the sole steward.

Stewardship may be shared, delegated, inherited, temporary, collective or jurisdictionally imposed.

Stewardship does not imply ownership.

Ownership does not erase stewardship obligations.

---

# 12. Obligation Mathematics

An obligation is a valid expectation that a participant perform, provide, refrain, preserve, return, compensate, maintain or otherwise satisfy a defined requirement.

An obligation state may be represented as:

`O = {issuer, obligor, beneficiary, object, condition, due-time, status, evidence, authority}`

Obligation status can evolve through:

`proposed → authorized → active → satisfied | modified | transferred | disputed | expired | breached | remediated`

### Obligation balance

Where commensurable:

`O_remaining = O_total − O_fulfilled − O_released`

Unfulfilled obligation is not necessarily debt.

Debt is one legally and economically specific form of obligation.

### Obligation priority

Where multiple obligations compete for limited capacity, priority may depend on:

`legality + urgency + harm prevention + dependency + vulnerability + explicit agreement + jurisdiction + reversibility`

A numerical optimization cannot override a mandatory legal or constitutional constraint.

---

# 13. Reciprocity Doctrine

Reciprocity is the relationship between giving, receiving, enabling, protecting, maintaining and returning across one or more participants over time.

Reciprocity does not require immediate or equal exchange.

A reciprocity relation may be represented as a directed network rather than a pairwise ledger:

`A → B → C → A`

or:

`A → ecosystem → B`

The relevant property is **recognized balance of obligations and returns**, not merely same-day equality.

### Reciprocity invariants

1. Equal contribution is not required for legitimate reciprocity.
2. Equal return is not required for legitimate reciprocity.
3. Reciprocity may be delayed.
4. Reciprocity may be distributed across relationships.
5. Coercion invalidates the assumption of free reciprocity where the applicable consent model requires voluntariness.
6. Care, public goods and stewardship may create legitimate asymmetrical reciprocity.

---

# 14. Reputation Dynamics

Reputation is the accumulated public or authorized expectation produced by attributed evidence about past behavior, contribution, reliability, integrity or outcomes.

Reputation is distinct from trust:

`Reputation = evidence about observed history`

`Trust = contextual expectation about future reliability`

A reputation state may be modeled as:

`Rep_t = G(history, recency, significance, independence, context, dispute, correction)`

Reputation must preserve:

- attribution;
- provenance;
- time;
- context;
- evidence quality;
- corrections;
- disputes;
- resolution state.

### Reputation dynamics

A system SHOULD avoid naive additive scoring. One model may use weighted evidence:

`Rep_new = normalize(Σ w_i e_i)`

where `w_i` reflects relevance, recency, reliability and context.

The exact function is domain-specific.

Reputation must not become an irreversible identity sentence. Valid correction and redemption must remain mathematically representable.

Reputation does not confer authority.

# 15. Continuity Economics

Continuity economics measures the value and obligation associated with maintaining the ability of a participant, relationship, organization, territory or ecosystem to continue functioning through time.

Continuity includes:

`maintenance + resilience + reserves + knowledge preservation + succession + recovery + interoperability + redundancy + repairability`

A continuity state may be represented as:

`K(t) = capability to sustain required functions across future states`

Continuity cost is not the same as present operating cost.

`Total continuity burden = present maintenance + expected disruption cost + recovery cost + dependency risk`

Investments that lower future disruption can create positive value even without immediate monetary return.

### Continuity principle

A system that maximizes present output while destroying its future operating capacity is not in equilibrium.

---

# 16. Civilization Economics

Civilization economics extends economic measurement from individual transactions to networked continuity across households, organizations, institutions, territories and ecosystems.

Civilization-scale quantities include:

`infrastructure + knowledge + trust + health + education + productive capacity + ecological viability + social coordination + institutional legitimacy + continuity`

A civilization economy should therefore measure both:

`flow` — what moves through the system;

and

`stock` — what the system preserves as future capacity.

A system can show strong short-term flow while degrading stock.

Therefore:

`Civilization health ≠ transaction volume`

and

`Growth ≠ improvement`.

### Civilization balance

A robust model considers at least:

`productive capacity − degradation + regeneration + learning + resilience`

with domain-specific measurement for each term.

Externalized harms must not disappear from the model merely because they are borne by another participant, territory or generation.

# 17. Universal Equilibrium

Universal equilibrium is not a static point where every quantity is equal.

It is a dynamically maintained state in which:

- legitimate obligations remain satisfiable;
- essential resources remain viable;
- contribution and recognized return remain coherent;
- trust remains within healthy bounds;
- relationships remain compatible with their declared terms;
- continuity capacity is preserved;
- residuals remain recoverable where feasible;
- authority and capability remain properly bounded;
- evidence remains sufficient for consequential decisions;
- the system can adapt without violating constitutional invariants.

A generalized equilibrium condition can be expressed as:

`E = f(C, R, O, T, Rel, Res, S, K, P, B)`

where:

- `C` = contribution;
- `R` = return;
- `O` = obligation state;
- `T` = trust;
- `Rel` = relationship state;
- `Res` = resource state;
- `S` = stewardship;
- `K` = continuity capacity;
- `P` = Pulse/change;
- `B` = Becoming/learning state.

Equilibrium exists when relevant constraints and target conditions are satisfied, not when every variable is equal.

### Disequilibrium

Disequilibrium is actionable information.

`Deviation → diagnosis → authorized response → measurement → learning`

The system must not automatically punish every deviation. Some deviations are innovation, transition, recovery or necessary adaptation.

# 18. Constitutional Invariants

The following invariants govern use of constitutional mathematics:

1. **Context invariant:** no material quantity is complete without context.
2. **Identity invariant:** a measurement does not redefine the identity of its subject.
3. **Authority invariant:** mathematical optimization never creates authority.
4. **Consent invariant:** consent cannot be inferred from a score.
5. **Representation invariant:** a model of reality is not automatically reality itself.
6. **Evidence invariant:** consequential claims preserve provenance and epistemic status.
7. **Unit invariant:** quantities may be combined only when their units and semantic bases permit it.
8. **Time invariant:** dynamic quantities retain temporal validity.
9. **Relationship invariant:** relationship context cannot be silently discarded when it affects interpretation.
10. **Non-collapse invariant:** distinct concepts such as value, trust, reputation, authority, ownership, stewardship and capability must not be collapsed into one score.
11. **Human dignity invariant:** human worth, rights and legitimacy are never defined by economic or reputational scores.
12. **Participant diversity invariant:** mathematical models must remain valid for participant types whose semantics differ.
13. **Continuity invariant:** present optimization must not silently destroy required future capacity.
14. **Externality invariant:** consequential harms cannot be erased merely because another participant bears them.
15. **Reciprocity invariant:** legitimate reciprocity need not be immediate or numerically equal.
16. **Scarcity invariant:** scarcity is contextual and does not automatically justify exclusion or extraction.
17. **Abundance invariant:** abundance is not a mandate for wasteful consumption.
18. **Stewardship invariant:** stewardship remains distinct from ownership.
19. **Obligation invariant:** obligation remains attributable to an issuer, obligor, beneficiary/object and valid authority context.
20. **Trust invariant:** trust is contextual confidence, not truth, consent or authority.
21. **Reputation invariant:** reputation records history; it does not dictate immutable future identity.
22. **Pulse invariant:** Pulse is movement/feedback, not currency or authority.
23. **Becoming invariant:** learning and transformation preserve provenance and do not erase the history that produced them.
24. **Audit invariant:** consequential calculations must be reproducible to the extent appropriate to their risk.
25. **Reversibility invariant:** where errors can materially harm a participant, models SHOULD preserve correction, dispute, rollback or remediation paths.
26. **No-single-score invariant:** no universal scalar may become the hidden substitute for constitutional judgment.

# 21. Relationship to Existing Economic Doctrine

OMNII Constitutional Mathematics does not replace Carbon Actual economic vocabulary or the existing economic ontology.

The existing economic ontology already establishes vocabulary for Value, Pulse, value differential, logical ledgers, resource/capacity flow, no-waste/Ash, continuity/maintenance and legitimate monetization. It also explicitly leaves several formulas and legal treatments open for empirical, legal and security review.

The role of this document is narrower and more foundational:

`Constitutional Semantics → Constitutional Mathematics → domain economics / accounting / finance implementations`

Therefore:

- accounting remains an implementation and compliance discipline;
- finance remains a domain of economic practice;
- monetary systems remain jurisdiction- and policy-specific;
- tokenization remains an implementation option;
- economic formulas remain testable rather than sacred;
- mathematical models may be refined without redefining the architecture.
