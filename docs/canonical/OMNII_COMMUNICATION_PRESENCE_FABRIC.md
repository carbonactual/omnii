# OMNII Communication & Presence Fabric

**Status:** CANONICAL CAPABILITY SCOPE
**Scope:** Ecosystem-wide; historical → current → emerging → future
**Parent:** OMNII ecosystem / capability fabric
**Intelligence:** ABBA orchestration

## 1. Purpose

Communication is an ecosystem capability, not a product feature. Products and operating environments are faces/services that consume the shared Communication & Presence Fabric. The bulk of communication, presence, identity/representation, session intelligence, provenance, privacy, routing, safety and continuity capability belongs in the ecosystem.

A product may add only the exact behavior, workflow and presentation unique to that product.

The governing flow is:

```text
IDENTITY / AUTHORITY
→ PRESENCE / REPRESENTATION
→ COMMUNICATION
→ SESSION / INTERACTION
→ RECORD / PROVENANCE
→ CONTINUITY / MEMORY
→ GOVERNED ECOSYSTEM FEEDBACK
→ CURATION / ADAPTATION
```

## 2. Identity is not appearance

The ecosystem maintains a canonical identity and authority relationship independently of how an entity appears.

A verified entity may communicate using:

- real face;
- real voice;
- avatar;
- anime/stylized character;
- synthetic or generated face;
- synthetic/modified voice;
- AI representative;
- text-only identity;
- other approved representations.

A representation does not itself establish identity. Where a representation is synthetic, cloned, delegated or transformed, its provenance, authorization and disclosure state must be governed.

## 3. Persona, privacy and incognito modes

Communication can expose the whole identity, a bounded identity, or an approved persona.

Supported conceptual modes include:

- public identity;
- private identity;
- restricted identity;
- pseudonymous identity;
- anonymous interaction where the use case permits;
- incognito persona;
- professional persona;
- family persona;
- community persona;
- role/persona-specific presentation;
- delegated identity/agent representation.

Privacy-preserving communication may minimize what the counterparty learns without breaking the canonical identity/authority relationship behind the session. Selective disclosure and verifiable presentations are preferred over duplicating identity systems.

- wired networks;
- radio;

## 7. Presence

Presence includes:

- online/offline/available/busy states;
- location/proximity where authorized;
- session participation;
- real-time appearance;
- avatar/character state;
- speaking state;
- device state;
- accessibility state;
- environmental context;
- spatial position;
- presence history where lawfully and explicitly retained.

Presence is a capability and signal, not blanket permission to track a person.

## 9. Tracking, telemetry and anti-tracking

Tracking is a governed capability, not a default right.

The fabric may support, where authorized:

- delivery/read state;
- presence state;
- session telemetry;
- device telemetry;
- network/session observability;
- location/proximity;
- attendance tracking;
- security tracing;
- operational tracing;
- incident reconstruction;
- fraud/abuse detection;
- anomaly detection;
- provenance and audit trails.

It must also support:

- tracking controls;
- consent controls;
- discovery controls;
- metadata minimization;
- privacy-preserving routing;
- anti-tracking modes;
- retention/expiry controls;
- selective disclosure;
- separation of operational telemetry from identity unless authorized.

## 10. Grey / covert / anomalous communication

The ecosystem recognizes communication that is indirect, ambient, low-visibility, unintended or concealed.

Examples include:

- ambient signals;
- side-channel-like system behavior;
- covert-channel indicators;
- steganographic content;
- anomalous communication paths;
- out-of-band communication;
- informal human signals.

The ecosystem may detect, classify and protect against unauthorized/covert channels. It does not make covert evasion an ecosystem service.

## 11. Synthetic, cloned and delegated presence

The fabric supports a clear distinction between:

```text
ORIGINAL IDENTITY
→ AUTHORIZATION
→ REPRESENTATION
→ COMMUNICATION
```

Possible representations include:

- avatar clone;
- voice clone;
- visual clone;
- animated character;
- AI-controlled delegate;
- organization representative;
- role-based digital representative.

The system should preserve:

- who authorized the representation;
- what it is permitted to do;
- whether a human is currently controlling it;
- whether it is autonomous within a bounded scope;
- whether the media is AI-generated or modified;
- provenance and integrity signals;
- disclosure requirements;
- revocation status.

## 12. Provenance and authenticity

Communication artifacts may require provenance for:

- identity/representation;
- source;
- creation;
- modification;
- AI generation;
- AI transformation;
- recording;
- signatures;
- timestamps;
- chain of custody.

Where applicable, cryptographically signed and tamper-evident content provenance is preferred.

## 16. Continuum

Communication is part of the ecosystem continuum.

Where authorized, interaction outputs may update:

- knowledge;
- memory;
- relationships;
- registries;
- commitments;
- tasks;
- value/pulse signals;
- service outcomes;

The ecosystem should learn from communication without assuming that everything communicated is globally reusable. Privacy, consent, authority, provenance and retention govern reuse.

## 17. Agent communication

A2A-style agent interoperability is treated as a communication capability rather than a separate ecosystem.

Agents may:

- discover capabilities;
- negotiate interaction modalities;
- exchange tasks;
- stream results;
- operate asynchronously;
- involve humans;
- hand work across agents;
- use tools/services through other ecosystem capabilities.

MCP, A2A and other protocols are interchangeable implementation mechanisms under the canonical communication/capability layer; they do not become competing ecosystem primitives.

## 19. Maturity

Every communication capability is classified as one of:

- Canonical
- Implemented
- Emerging
- Candidate / Experimental
- Future
- Deprecated

The registry records maturity independently from product availability.

## 20. Non-goals

The ecosystem does not:

- create isolated communication stacks per product;
- equate appearance with identity;
- equate tracking with ownership of a person;
- create unauthorized covert-communication services;
- force future modalities into present production;
- make any single protocol or vendor the permanent primitive;
- turn communication artifacts into ungoverned global memory.

## 21. External anchors

The scope aligns conceptually with current standards and research including:

- W3C Verifiable Credentials 2.0 and verifiable presentations/selective persona disclosure;
- A2A for interoperable agent communication, capability discovery and task collaboration;
- C2PA Content Credentials for provenance of generated/modified media;
- ITU work on immersive live experience and integrated sensing + communication;
- privacy-preserving communications patterns that separate contactability from direct disclosure of identifiers.

These references are implementation anchors, not authority over ecosystem-native law.
