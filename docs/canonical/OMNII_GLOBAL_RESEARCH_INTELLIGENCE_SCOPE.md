# OMNII — Global Research Intelligence Capability

**Status:** CANONICAL CAPABILITY SCOPE  
**Scope:** Global-by-default  
**Layer:** Shared ecosystem research, evidence synthesis and knowledge-discovery capability

## 1. Constitutional definition

**Global Research Intelligence** is the reusable ecosystem capability for finding, collecting, comparing, synthesizing and contextualizing information needed to understand entities, domains, technologies, markets, rules, opportunities, capabilities and changing conditions.

It answers:

> **“What do we need to know, what evidence exists, what does it mean, what remains unknown, and where should deeper investigation go next?”**

Research Intelligence is distinct from Market Intelligence: Market Intelligence specializes in changing market conditions; Research Intelligence is the broader evidence-discovery and synthesis capability across domains.

## 2. Global-by-default

Research can span:

```text
local → national → regional → continental → global
```

and across languages, jurisdictions, sectors, institutions and time periods, subject to lawful access and policy.

## 3. Research universe

Research may cover:

- organizations and people where lawful
- products and technologies
- manufacturing processes
- scientific and technical knowledge
- standards and regulations
- markets and industries
- public procurement
- infrastructure
- geography
- history and culture
- education and skills
- finance/economics
- environmental conditions
- transport and logistics
- institutional systems
- competitor and supplier landscapes
- policy changes
- patents and intellectual property
- academic/research outputs
- public records

## 4. Research lifecycle

```text
QUESTION
→ SCOPE
→ SOURCE DISCOVERY
→ RETRIEVAL
→ EXTRACTION
→ NORMALIZATION
→ CROSS-CHECK
→ SYNTHESIS
→ UNCERTAINTY
→ FINDINGS
→ CITABLE EVIDENCE
→ DECISION/USE
→ UPDATE
```

## 5. Evidence model

Research records preserve:

```text
source
publisher/owner
publication date
observation date
retrieval date
scope
jurisdiction
claim
supporting evidence
confidence
contradictions
freshness
provenance
```

The capability must preserve disagreement rather than manufacture false consensus.

## 6. Source classes

Authorized sources may include:

- government and regulator publications
- registries
- standards organizations
- academic literature
- institutional reports
- company disclosures
- trade and professional associations
- public datasets
- participant submissions
- licensed databases
- direct interviews/field research
- ecosystem records
- permitted web sources

Source authority is contextual. A primary source is not automatically correct; a secondary source is not automatically weak.

## 7. Entity and claim normalization

The engine should resolve:

- aliases
- duplicate entities
- inconsistent naming
- units
- currencies
- dates
- geography
- product categories
- terminology
- translated terms

It must preserve original source wording and the normalized representation.

## 8. Comparative research

Support:

- entity comparison
- product comparison
- technology comparison
- jurisdiction comparison
- supplier comparison
- historical comparison
- policy comparison
- market comparison
- implementation-pattern comparison

Differences in scope and evidence must remain visible.

## 9. Research briefs and dossiers

The capability can produce structured research objects containing:

```text
question
scope
key findings
source set
supporting claims
contradictions
unknowns
assumptions
risk flags
implications
recommended next research
```

## 10. Deep research and branching

A research question may branch into dependent questions:

```text
Question
├─ entity identification
├─ capability verification
├─ regulatory condition
├─ market condition
├─ technology condition
└─ economic feasibility
```

The system should track dependencies and avoid repeatedly answering the same sub-question.

## 11. Monitoring and change detection

Research topics can become monitored objects with alerts for:

- new publications
- changed regulations
- new market data
- new suppliers
- new technologies
- changed prices
- changed capabilities
- newly discovered evidence
- contradictory evidence

## 12. Knowledge boundaries

Research output must distinguish:

```text
FACT / OBSERVATION
INTERPRETATION
INFERENCE
ESTIMATE
FORECAST
HYPOTHESIS
UNKNOWN
```

AI synthesis does not create primary evidence merely by being plausible.

## 13. Research-to-action handoff

Research may feed:

```text
research finding
→ sourcing requirement
→ opportunity signal
→ matching candidate
→ market intelligence update
→ workflow
```

The handoff preserves provenance and confidence.

## 14. Privacy and access

Research respects:

- access permissions
- privacy
- confidentiality
- purpose limitation
- licensing constraints
- jurisdictional restrictions
- source terms

Private information is not made globally discoverable merely because it is useful.

## 15. Global language and localization

Support multilingual research while preserving:

- original language
- translation
- translation confidence where relevant
- local terminology
- cultural context
- jurisdiction-specific meanings

Localization changes presentation/context, not canonical semantics.

## 16. Ecosystem interfaces

Consumes/contributes to:

- Knowledge
- Memory/continuity
- Entity/Identity
- Global Market Intelligence
- Global Sourcing
- Global Opportunity
- Global Matching & Discovery Intelligence
- Standards & Conformance
- Evidence/Provenance
- Actual / Atlas / Vault
- Pulse
- ABBA

## 17. Non-goals

This capability does not become:

- an authority that determines truth by itself
- a regulator
- a surveillance system
- a replacement for expert/legal advice where required
- the canonical source of every external fact

## 18. Canonical invariant

> **Research Intelligence organizes and synthesizes evidence across the world without confusing discovery, interpretation, inference and authority.**
