# OMNII — Global Market Intelligence Capability

**Status:** CANONICAL CAPABILITY SCOPE  
**Scope:** Global-by-default  
**Layer:** Shared ecosystem intelligence; consumed by Sourcing, Opportunity, Matching, Desk, TIP and institutional systems

## 1. Constitutional definition

**Global Market Intelligence** is the reusable ecosystem capability for understanding changing market conditions that affect demand, supply, price, capacity, competition, trade, access, risk and value across jurisdictions.

It answers:

> **“What is happening in the relevant market, what is changing, what evidence supports that view, and how does it affect the available paths?”**

Market Intelligence observes and interprets market conditions. It does not become the market, regulator, exchange, marketplace or financial adviser.

## 2. Global-by-default

Market intelligence can compare:

```text
local
→ territorial
→ national
→ regional
→ continental
→ intercontinental
→ global
```

Geography is a dimension, not an architectural boundary. Jurisdiction-specific rules remain attached to the relevant context.

## 6. Price intelligence

Support:

- observed prices
- price ranges
- historical movement
- geographic differences
- wholesale/retail differences
- production/input costs where known
- landed-cost comparisons
- price volatility
- price anomalies

Headline prices must not be presented as equivalent when quality, quantity, terms, delivery basis, tax, duty, finance or other material conditions differ.

## 7. Demand intelligence

Identify:

- confirmed demand
- recurring demand
- emerging demand
- unmet demand
- seasonal demand
- geographic demand gaps
- institutional demand
- procurement demand
- aggregated demand

Demand inference must be labelled separately from confirmed orders or participant commitments.

## 8. Supply and capacity intelligence

Represent:

- available supply
- future supply
- idle capacity
- constrained capacity
- inventory surplus
- shortages
- production expansion
- facility openings/closures
- machinery availability

This contributes signals to Sourcing, Opportunity and Matching but does not replace the canonical Supply & Capacity Graph.

## 9. Competition and substitution intelligence

Track:

- alternative suppliers
- alternative manufacturers
- competing products/services
- technology substitutes
- local substitutes
- used/refurbished alternatives
- process alternatives
- geographic alternatives

The system must distinguish documented competition from inferred similarity.

## 10. Trade and market-access intelligence

Market conditions may include:

- import/export patterns
- tariffs and duties
- non-tariff barriers
- customs friction
- standards requirements
- certification needs
- restricted/prohibited conditions
- currency/settlement constraints
- corridor reliability
- market-entry requirements

These are intelligence inputs. Regulated execution remains with the appropriate authorities and providers.

## 11. Market opportunity signals

The capability can surface signals such as:

```text
rising demand
falling supply
persistent price gap
new buyer geography
capacity surplus
new technology
new procurement demand
import dependence
local production gap
new market access
supplier concentration risk
```

These signals may feed Global Opportunity but do not automatically become confirmed opportunities.

## 12. Market risk intelligence

Represent:

- volatility
- supply concentration
- dependency risk
- geopolitical/jurisdictional exposure
- logistics disruption
- regulatory change
- currency risk
- demand uncertainty
- technology obsolescence
- counterparty concentration
- quality uncertainty

Risk indicators are evidence-backed and scenario-aware.

## 13. Forecasting and scenarios

The capability may produce:

- trend estimates
- demand scenarios
- price scenarios
- capacity scenarios
- market-entry scenarios
- disruption scenarios
- alternative-path scenarios

Forecasts are not facts. Assumptions, uncertainty, timeframe and evidence must remain visible.

## 14. Market comparison

The engine can compare markets using relevant dimensions:

```text
addressable demand
competition
price/value
input availability
logistics
regulation
finance
risk
capacity
market access
strategic fit
```

No single country or region is constitutionally preferred.

## 15. Market-entry intelligence

For a product, service, factory or participant, identify:

- potentially suitable markets
- required standards
- buyer/channel structure
- local competitors
- expected logistics path
- financing constraints
- partner requirements
- evidence gaps
- market-entry risks

Actual market entry remains a separately authorized activity.

## 16. Intelligence freshness

Dynamic intelligence carries:

- observation time
- effective period
- expiry/freshness rule
- source
- confidence
- revision history
- geographic scope
- product/category scope

Stale intelligence must not be presented as current evidence.

## 17. Evidence hierarchy

Use explicit states:

```text
OBSERVED
VERIFIED
REPORTED
ESTIMATED
INFERRED
FORECAST
SCENARIO
UNVERIFIED
OUTDATED
UNAVAILABLE
```

AI-generated conclusions are never silently promoted to observed fact.

- price

- demand
- capacity
- shipping
- regulation
- procurement
- supplier performance
- technology

Changes may trigger re-ranking in Sourcing, Opportunity or Matching.

## 20. Non-goals

Global Market Intelligence does not become:

- a stock/commodity exchange
- a marketplace
- a regulator
- a customs authority
- a bank
- a private rating authority
- a guaranteed forecasting oracle

It is the ecosystem's **market-condition intelligence layer**.

## 21. Canonical invariant

> **Market Intelligence describes and interprets changing external market conditions with explicit evidence, freshness and uncertainty so other ecosystem capabilities can make better choices.**
