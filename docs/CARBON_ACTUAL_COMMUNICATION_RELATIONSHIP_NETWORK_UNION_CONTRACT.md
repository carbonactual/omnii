# Carbon Actual Communication, Relationship, Network, Union & Commitment Contract

**Status:** Canonical kernel contract

Carbon Actual requires one universal social and coordination fabric for humans, intelligences, organizations, machines, communities, products and future participant classes.

## 1. Communication

Communication is the exchange of messages, signals, meaning, requests, instructions, evidence or state between participants.

Communication may be synchronous or asynchronous and may occur through any authorized medium.

Communication does not imply relationship, trust, authority or commitment.

## 2. Message

Every material message should preserve, where applicable:

- sender
- intended recipient/audience
- content or reference
- purpose
- time
- context
- authorization
- integrity
- confidentiality class
- delivery state
- acknowledgement state
- provenance

## 3. Connection

A Connection is an established communication or interaction pathway between participants.

A connection may be:

- direct
- mediated
- temporary
- persistent
- conditional
- public
- private
- delegated
- machine-to-machine
- human-to-intelligence
- human-to-human
- intelligence-to-intelligence
- organization-to-organization

Connection does not automatically grant access to all information or capabilities.

## 4. Relationship

A Relationship is a recognized association between participants, objects or contexts.

Relationships may represent:

- family
- friendship
- employment
- membership
- ownership
- partnership
- customer/provider
- teacher/student
- patient/care provider
- government/citizen
- agent/principal
- team membership
- dependency
- competition
- community
- affiliation

Every relationship should have scope and applicable authority where relevant.

## 5. Network

A Network is a connected structure of participants and relationships.

Networks are not merely social graphs. They can represent economic, technical, institutional, knowledge, logistics, communication and capability relationships.

A participant may belong to many networks simultaneously.

## 6. Union

Union is the governed mechanism through which participants, capabilities, resources or networks become connected for a shared purpose without requiring them to lose their individual identity.

Union does not mean merger.

```text
CONNECTION → RELATIONSHIP → NETWORK → UNION
```

These are progressively stronger forms of coordination, not interchangeable labels.

## 7. Commitment

A Commitment is an accepted intention or obligation to perform, provide, maintain, support, refrain or participate under defined conditions.

Commitments should identify:

- committing party
- beneficiary/affected parties
- intended outcome
- scope
- conditions
- time window
- required resources
- authority
- evidence of acceptance
- completion criteria
- cancellation/release conditions

## 8. Coordination

Coordination aligns participants toward one or more outcomes.

Coordination can occur without a formal relationship or commitment.

## 9. Collaboration

Collaboration is coordinated contribution toward a shared outcome.

Collaboration may create commitments, contracts, value contributions and Pulse.

## 10. Team

A Team is an execution composition assembled for a defined outcome.

It may contain humans, intelligences, software, hardware, organizations, services and resources.

Team membership is contextual and does not necessarily change the underlying identity of participants.

## 11. Swarm

A Swarm is a set of alternative or complementary implementations/capabilities that the ecosystem evaluates collectively.

The Swarm enables:

- capability comparison
- strength/weakness analysis
- redundancy
- substitution
- research
- interoperability
- continuous improvement

A Swarm is not required to have a single owner or a single implementation.

## 12. Team formation from Swarms

```text
OUTCOME
 ↓
REQUIREMENTS
 ↓
SWARMS
 ↓
CAPABILITY SELECTION
 ↓
TEAM COMPOSITION
 ↓
COMMITMENTS
 ↓
WORKFLOW
```

This preserves the LEGO principle: products select and compose existing capabilities instead of rebuilding them.

## 13. Communication authority

A communication pathway must not be treated as authority to act.

Receiving a message is not equivalent to receiving permission.

## 14. Consent and relationship boundaries

Relationships may establish context, but they do not automatically grant unlimited consent or access.

Sensitive interactions must resolve applicable privacy, authorization and policy requirements.

## 15. Identity continuity

Participants retain their identity while moving across networks, teams, products and integrations.

The ecosystem should prefer portable identity and delegated authorization rather than repeated account creation.

## 16. Network effects

The value of a network may increase through useful connections, complementary capabilities, trust, shared knowledge and coordination. Network growth must not be treated as inherently beneficial; low-quality, malicious or irrelevant connections can reduce ecosystem value.

## 17. Communication memory

Material communications can become evidence, relationship history, organizational memory or Pulse according to policy. They should not automatically become permanent memory.

## 18. Commitment lifecycle

```text
PROPOSED → REVIEWED → ACCEPTED → ACTIVE → FULFILLED
                         ↓
                  SUSPENDED / MODIFIED
                         ↓
                    RELEASED / EXPIRED
```

Disputed commitments require a separate dispute state rather than silently marking them fulfilled or failed.

## 19. Failure and continuity

Communication systems should support acknowledgement, retry, fallback channels, offline operation where appropriate, escalation and continuity.

A failed communication attempt is itself an observable event and may generate Pulse without being treated as successful communication.

## 20. Universal rule

Products such as government systems, education, property, transport, logistics, marketplaces, human-resource systems, HAPI World and community systems should inherit this social/coordination fabric instead of creating separate relationship and communication primitives.
