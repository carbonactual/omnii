# Carbon Actual Universal Orchestration Engine

**Status:** Canonical architecture contract

ABBA orchestrates the ecosystem by resolving intent into authorized, contextual, resource-aware execution.

## Universal loop

```text
SIGNAL / NEED / INTENT
        ↓
CONTEXT RESOLUTION
        ↓
OUTCOME DEFINITION
        ↓
REQUIREMENT DECOMPOSITION
        ↓
CAPABILITY DISCOVERY
        ↓
SWARM EVALUATION
        ↓
RESOURCE + CAPACITY + AVAILABILITY
        ↓
AUTHORITY + CONSENT + CONSTRAINT CHECK
        ↓
TEAM COMPOSITION
        ↓
WORKFLOW + SCHEDULING
        ↓
EXECUTION
        ↓
PULSE / OBSERVATION
        ↓
VALUE / OUTCOME EVALUATION
        ↓
SETTLEMENT / RESPONSIBILITY
        ↓
LEARNING
        ↓
BECOMING / NEXT CURATION
```

## Resolution principle

ABBA should resolve the smallest sufficient composition capable of achieving the desired outcome under the applicable constraints.

Do not assemble a larger system merely because more components are available.

## Human-first execution boundary

If a capable human can effectively perform an action and the action is appropriate for human participation, the ecosystem should prefer enabling and coordinating that human rather than automatically displacing them with intelligence.

This is a design principle, not an absolute rule. Safety, accessibility, human preference, economics and task characteristics may change the optimal implementation.

## Intelligence boundary

Intelligence should augment, coordinate, calculate, search, simulate, automate and orchestrate where useful, while preserving declared human authority and consent boundaries.

## Resource resolution

The orchestrator must account for:

- capability
- capacity
- availability
- authority
- constraints
- dependencies
- risk
- cost/value
- continuity
- timing

## Swarm resolution

A Swarm is evaluated contextually rather than globally ranked.

Selection may consider:

- performance
- reliability
- privacy
- security
- openness
- interoperability
- cost
- latency
- availability
- locality
- maturity
- sustainability
- user preference

## Team resolution

A Team is generated from selected capability implementations and explicit roles.

Team composition may include humans, intelligences, software, hardware, organizations and resources in any combination.

## Execution modes

The orchestrator supports:

- recommend
- request confirmation
- execute automatically within authority
- delegate
- escalate to human
- simulate
- defer
- refuse
- recover

## Refusal

The system must be able to refuse or pause an action when authority, safety, policy, identity, provenance, privacy or required evidence is insufficient.

## Escalation

Escalation routes unresolved decisions to the appropriate authority rather than silently guessing.

## Learning

Every completed workflow produces structured learning inputs without rewriting historical truth.

Learning may improve:

- capability evaluation
- Swarm selection
- scheduling
- resource allocation
- risk detection
- curation
- future opportunity matching

## No hidden commitments

Recommendations, simulations and forecasts must not silently become contracts, purchases, commitments, permissions or obligations.

## Product consequence

A product branch should primarily define its outcomes, policies, domain objects and interfaces. The universal orchestration engine supplies the common machinery for discovery, composition, allocation, execution, evaluation and learning.
