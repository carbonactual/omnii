# TIP — Markets & Marketplaces Master Architecture

**Ecosystem:** Carbon Actual / OMNII  
**Product:** TIP — Trade, Investment & Value Markets  
**Status:** CANONICAL EXTENSION — approved  
**Purpose:** Establish a universal taxonomy and architecture for markets and marketplaces across financial, physical, commercial, service, capability, rights, infrastructure, environmental, digital and future economic activity.

---

## 1. Constitutional Definition

A **Market** is an organized or emergent mechanism through which supply, demand, value, rights, capabilities, capital, risk, access, capacity or obligations can be discovered, compared, allocated, exchanged, contracted, financed or settled.

A **Marketplace** is the participant-facing or machine-facing environment that enables discovery, matching, negotiation, transaction formation, access, fulfillment, settlement, evidence or related market activity.

An exchange, trading venue, auction, OTC network, procurement system, marketplace, P2P network, funding pool, subscription catalog, capability registry or agentic commerce network may therefore implement a market or marketplace without sharing the same legal or operational form.

TIP must not assume:

```text
MARKET = EXCHANGE
MARKETPLACE = E-COMMERCE
TRADE = BUY/SELL
```

These are subsets of a broader market architecture.

---

## 2. Market vs Marketplace vs Venue vs Mechanism

TIP separates four concepts:

**Market** — the economic arena/rule system.  
**Marketplace** — the interface/network connecting participants and opportunities.  
**Venue** — a specific place/system where transactions or matching occur.  
**Mechanism** — the rule used to match, allocate, price or contract.

Examples:

```text
Commodity market
  → commodity marketplace
  → exchange / OTC venue
  → auction / order-book / RFQ mechanism
```

```text
AI capability market
  → capability marketplace
  → API/MCP/agent venue
  → subscription / metered / auction mechanism
```

The same market may have multiple marketplaces and venues.

A marketplace may aggregate multiple external markets without becoming the market operator itself.

## 11. Primary, Secondary and Tertiary Markets

TIP must distinguish:

### Primary

Creation/issuance/sale of a new asset, security, project, contract or capability.

### Secondary

Resale, transfer, assignment or trading of an existing eligible position, claim, right or asset.

### Tertiary / downstream

Further reuse, financing, derivative exposure, repackaging, servicing, recovery, recycling or transformation of economic relationships.

Examples:

```text
NEW EQUIPMENT
 → SALE
 → LEASE
 → FINANCE
 → SECONDARY SALE
 → REFURBISH
 → REUSE
 → RECYCLE
```

## 23. Cross-Market AI

The General Market Intelligence Engine should compare opportunities across markets and ask:

> Where can authorized capital, capability, inventory, capacity, rights or effort create the greatest sustainable risk-adjusted value?

The optimal result can be:

- trade
- investment
- financing
- leasing
- subscription
- capacity reservation
- arbitrage
- hedging
- insurance
- marketplace listing
- acquisition
- partnership
- reuse
- no action

This is the bridge between TIP's market universe and its capital-allocation intelligence.

---

## 24. Canonical Rule

> **Every legitimate market is a coordination mechanism for value, while every marketplace is an access and transaction surface over that market. TIP must be capable of creating, connecting, aggregating, powering and participating in markets without requiring a new foundational architecture for each one.**

A new market or marketplace should normally require taxonomy registration, rule configuration, jurisdictional mapping and tested integration—not a new economic ontology.
