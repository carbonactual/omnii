# TIP — Universal & Frontier Market Completeness

**Ecosystem:** Carbon Actual / OMNII  
**Status:** CANONICAL DESIGN — completeness boundary  
**Purpose:** close market-form and economic-participation gaps without introducing a second economic ontology.

---

## 1. Constitutional invariant

> No new economic activity, market, participant or value form bypasses the existing identity → authority/rights → object → eligibility → market form → contract/position → fulfillment/settlement → reconciliation → measurement → Pulse/value stack.

Classification never creates ownership, legal personhood, transferability, market permission, regulatory approval or a right to transact.

All product implementations remain subordinate to Carbon Actual constitutional authority and consume shared ecosystem primitives rather than creating product-specific foundational semantics.

---

## 5. Informal ↔ formal continuity

TIP treats informality as an operating condition, not as absence of economic reality.

A participant may begin as:

```text
UNREGISTERED / INFORMAL
        ↓
IDENTIFIED
        ↓
PROFILED
        ↓
VERIFIED
        ↓
CERTIFIED / LICENSED where required
        ↓
FORMAL COUNTERPARTY
```

The system must preserve provenance and must never falsely represent an informal seller, worker, product or organization as formally licensed.

Offline, low-connectivity and assisted intake are first-class operational paths. Voice, SMS, USSD, agent-assisted capture, photographed documents and assisted verification can feed the same canonical records when properly evidenced.

The ILO's 2026 platform-economy standard reinforces that platform-mediated work can span formal and informal economies and requires explicit attention to worker protections and cross-border relationships.

## 6. Local → intra-territorial → regional → global

A market interaction may move through any spatial scale:

```text
PERSON
  ↕
NEIGHBOR
  ↕
COMMUNITY / MARKET
  ↕
CITY / LGA
  ↕
STATE / REGION
  ↕
NATIONAL
  ↕
CROSS-BORDER / REGIONAL
  ↕
INTERCONTINENTAL
  ↕
GLOBAL
```

The same object can therefore be locally sourced, nationally aggregated, regionally processed and globally sold without changing its identity.

Local priority is a matching policy, not a separate ontology: prefer local supply/workforce when quality, legality, total value, timing and risk are competitive.

## 7. Substitution and equivalence

The market model must support substitution rather than assuming an exact SKU is always required.

Examples:

- local equivalent for an import
- refurbished machine for a new machine
- processed input for raw material
- rail for road where available
- sea for air when urgency permits
- substitute material with approved specifications
- different supplier within the same standard tier
- service provider substitution
- barter counterpart substitution
- alternative financing source

Every substitution records the requirement, equivalence rule, differences, confidence and user/authority acceptance.

A single request can become a staged value chain:

```text
RAW
 ↓

 ↓
GRADED
 ↓
CLEANED
 ↓
PROCESSED
 ↓
SEMI-FINISHED
 ↓
FINISHED
 ↓
PACKED
 ↓
CERTIFIED
 ↓
STORED
 ↓
CONSOLIDATED
 ↓
MOVED
 ↓
DELIVERED
 ↓
USED
 ↓
RETURNED / RECOVERED
 ↓
SECONDARY USE
```

Every transformation may add value, cost, risk, labor, carbon, evidence and new market eligibility. The underlying object lineage remains traceable.

GS1 EPCIS provides a standards-based model for sharing the what/when/where/why/how of products and assets, including transformation, transport, sensor data, certification and chain-of-custody events. TIP should interoperate rather than invent an incompatible event vocabulary.

## 9. Machine and agent economies

Machines, robots, AI agents and software processes may participate as controlled economic actors where law and system policy permit.

They retain distinct fields for:

- principal
- owner/controller
- authority
- delegation
- capability
- limits
- provenance
- accountability
- credential
- execution history

No machine, robot or AI agent receives human legal personhood merely by classification.

Machine-to-machine procurement, autonomous restocking, agent negotiation and agent-to-agent settlement are prospective market forms that can be represented without asserting unrestricted autonomy.

## 12. Extra-terrestrial boundary vocabulary

The ontology may represent hypothetical discoveries or future scenarios using:

```text
UNKNOWN_NON_HUMAN_ACTOR
EXTRATERRESTRIAL_OBJECT
EXTRATERRESTRIAL_RESOURCE
UNKNOWN_EXTERNAL_SOURCE
```

These are boundary vocabulary only. They do not assert extraterrestrial life, intelligence, ownership, jurisdiction, property rights or commercial counterparties.

Any activation would require evidence, provenance, authorization and applicable law.

## 13. Maturity is separate from legal eligibility

```text
HISTORICAL
CURRENT
EMERGING
PROSPECTIVE
EXPERIMENTAL
SPECULATIVE
UNKNOWN
PROHIBITED
INVALID
```

Maturity answers “how established is this market/object?”

Legal eligibility answers “may this actor perform this action here, now, under this authority?”

Operational status answers “what is its current execution state?”

These dimensions must never be collapsed.

## 15. Canonical boundary

The TIP market universe is intentionally open-ended. New forms should normally be represented by composing existing primitives and registering new facets, object subclasses, relationships, eligibility rules or market-form descriptors.

A new product, marketplace or integration must not create a second transaction ontology merely because its commercial language is different.

**Canonical rule:** represent first; authorize second; transact third.

## 16. Evidence basis

Current interoperability and trade-facilitation references include:

- WTO Trade Facilitation Agreement — single window, risk management, post-clearance audit and technology-enabled trade facilitation.
- UN Trade and Development (UNCTAD) — electronic Single Window and ASYCUDA modernization.
- GS1 EPCIS/CBV — interoperable supply-chain event and traceability data.
- FIATA — digital multimodal bills of lading and digital freight forwarding.
- ILO Convention No. 193 (2026) — platform-economy work across formal/informal and cross-border settings.

These sources support interoperability and boundary design; they do not by themselves authorize any particular transaction in any jurisdiction.
