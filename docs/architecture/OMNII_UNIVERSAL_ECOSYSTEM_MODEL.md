# OMNII Universal Ecosystem Model

**Status: CANONICAL ARCHITECTURAL EXTENSION**

This document strengthens the canonical OMNII architecture. It does not create a competing architecture, replace the foundational kernel sequence, create a new phase, or make OMNI itself a constitutional layer.

## 1. Canonical Distinction

**OMNII** is the canonical repository and architecture of the ecosystem.

**OMNI** is an omnidirectional mode/state of ecosystem movement. It is not the architecture, repository, platform, product, AI, authority or privileged layer. OMNI is conceptually alongside phenomena and concepts such as mathematics, value, economics, knowledge, motion, time, trust, relationship and Pulse.

> **OMNII describes and implements the ecosystem; OMNI describes a way the ecosystem moves.**

## 2. Universal Reality Principle

OMNII must not assume that reality is software.

The ecosystem may contain and connect:

- humans;
- animals;
- plants and biological systems;
- AI and software agents;
- robots and autonomous systems;
- machines and hardware;
- physical and digital objects;
- organizations and institutions;
- families and communities;
- territories and environments;
- infrastructure and resources;
- processes, activities and events;
- abstract and conceptual entities;
- future or currently unknown forms of participation.

Universal architecture does **not** mean treating all of these as identical.

> **Universal participation does not imply universal equivalence.**

Every participant retains semantics appropriate to its nature, agency, capabilities, boundaries, relationships, authority, condition, context and lifecycle.

## 3. Universal Participant Envelope

The common OMNII participant envelope may include:

`identity + kind + nature + embodiment + representation + lifecycle + provenance + authority context + agency + capabilities + needs/interests where applicable + attributes + relationships + dependencies + resources + location + temporal existence + state + condition + uncertainty + extensions`

The envelope is universal; participant-specific profiles remain distinct.

A human is not an AI. An AI is not a robot. A robot is not an animal. A company is not a river. A building is not a transaction. They may nevertheless participate in the same universal graph.

## 4. Embodiment and Representation

Participants may be:

`PHYSICAL | DIGITAL | BIOLOGICAL | SOCIAL | INSTITUTIONAL | VIRTUAL | CYBERPHYSICAL | HYBRID`

A participant may have one or more representations, models, records, interfaces or digital twins.

> **A representation is not automatically the thing it represents.**

Representation is a first-class relationship. The architecture must preserve the distinction between a real-world entity, its digital representation, its simulation and any authorized action taken through that representation.

## 5. Agency, Capability and Authority

Agency, capability, permission and authority are distinct.

For any consequential participant or action, OMNII should be able to answer:

```text
WHAT CAN IT DO?
      ↓
WHAT MAY IT DO?
      ↓
WHO/WHAT AUTHORIZES IT?
      ↓
UNDER WHAT CONDITIONS?
      ↓
FOR HOW LONG?
      ↓
WHAT MUST BE RECORDED?
```

Autonomy does not imply sovereignty.

Intelligence does not imply legitimacy.

Capability does not imply permission.

Connectivity does not imply consent.

## 6. Authority and SEAL

Authority can be human, legal, institutional, delegated, regulatory, collective, constitutional, stewardship-based, guardianship-based or automated within explicit delegation, as applicable.

**SEAL** remains the explicit approval/consent/release mechanism where human or legally/constitutionally valid consent is required. SEAL does not replace authentication, authorization, policy, governance, guardianship or stewardship.

`Authentication → Authorization/Policy → SEAL where required → Execution within delegated boundaries`

No system may infer required consent from capability, optimization, silence, convenience or prior activity.

## 7. Stewardship, Ownership and Responsibility

Not everything that can be governed is owned.

OMNII must distinguish:

`OWNERSHIP | CUSTODY | STEWARDSHIP | GUARDIANSHIP | RESPONSIBILITY | JURISDICTION | AUTHORITY`

This distinction is essential for animals, ecosystems, public infrastructure, cultural assets, shared resources, future generations and other realities where ownership is not the correct relationship.

## 8. Time, Space and Context

Time and space are universal dimensions.

Participants and events may carry:

`origin + location + duration + temporal validity + movement + boundary + environment + jurisdiction + cultural context + lifecycle`

OMNII must preserve historical, current, projected and simulated states while preserving their epistemic status.

## 9. Observation and Actuation

OMNII explicitly models the relationship between reality, observation and action:

```text
OBSERVE
   ↓
INTERPRET
   ↓
PROPOSE / DECIDE
   ↓
AUTHORIZE
   ↓
ACTUATE
   ↓
MEASURE
   ↓
EVALUATE
   ↓
LEARN
```

Humans, animals, sensors, AI, agents, robots, machines and institutions may provide observations or signals according to their nature and capability.

Humans, robots, software, machines and institutions may act where authorized.

Observation is not automatically truth. Actuation is not automatically authority.

## 10. Knowledge and Uncertainty

OMNII must distinguish information from knowledge and assertion from fact.

Where material, claims should preserve epistemic status such as:

`OBSERVED | VERIFIED | INFERRED | PREDICTED | SIMULATED | REPORTED | CLAIMED | DISPUTED | UNKNOWN`

Relevant metadata may include:

`source + provenance + confidence + method + scope + timestamp + authority`

ABBA must reason about both the content of knowledge and the status of that knowledge.

## 11. Condition and Well-being

A universal **Condition** concept allows OMNII to represent the state of different kinds of participants without forcing one domain's definition onto another.

Depending on participant type, condition may include:

`health + welfare + safety + integrity + operability + stability + resilience + viability + fitness + quality`

This supports humans, animals, machines, infrastructure, organizations, environments and ecosystems while allowing domain-specific semantics.

## 12. Universal Graph

The universal graph remains:

`Participant --[typed Relationship]--> Participant`

Important relationship classes may include:

`represents, embodied-by, located-in, part-of, owns, stewards, governs, authorizes, delegates, observes, actuates, depends-on, provides, consumes, affects, communicates-with, related-to, derived-from, caused-by, transforms-into`

Ownership, custody, stewardship, guardianship, responsibility and jurisdiction must remain semantically distinct.

No product-specific graph may silently become an alternate universal graph.

## 13. Pulse, OMNI and Becoming

These three concepts should be explicitly distinguished:

```text
PULSE
= consequential movement/change

OMNI
= omnidirectional mode of ecosystem movement

BECOMING
= transformation produced through movement, evidence and learning
```

Pulse may arise from activity, movement, relationship changes, communication, transactions, proofs, observations, events, state transitions or other consequential changes as defined by applicable contracts.

Pulse is not currency, permission, authority or a substitute for event/state schemas.

OMNI does not mean uncontrolled movement. Omnidirectional movement remains constrained by identity, relationship, authority, boundaries, capabilities, resources, safety, jurisdiction, purpose and state.

## 14. Universal Reality Lifecycle

A generalized lifecycle applicable across participant categories is:

```text
EXIST
  ↓
IDENTIFY
  ↓
LOCATE / CONTEXTUALIZE
  ↓
RELATE
  ↓
OBSERVE
  ↓
UNDERSTAND
  ↓
INTEND
  ↓
AUTHORIZE
  ↓
ACT / RESPOND
  ↓
CHANGE STATE
  ↓
PRODUCE EVIDENCE
  ↓
LEARN
  ↓
BECOME
  ↺
```

This is a semantic model, not a mandatory linear workflow. OMNI permits valid movement across compatible directions and dimensions.

## 15. Physical–Digital Continuity

Physical and digital are manifestation domains, not separate constitutional universes.

The architecture must support mappings such as:

```text
Human ↔ Digital Identity
Robot ↔ Digital Twin
Building ↔ Property Record / Digital Model
Animal ↔ Animal Registry / Welfare Record
Vehicle ↔ Physical Asset + Digital Representation
Organization ↔ Legal / Operational Representation
Machine ↔ Telemetry + Operational State
Environment ↔ Spatial / Ecological Representation
```

The mapping must preserve identity, provenance, state, authority and representation boundaries.

## 16. Human, Animal and More-than-Human Participation

Humans remain first-class participants with agency, consent, authority, privacy, relationships and continuity.

Animals, plants, biological systems and ecosystems are also first-class participants where the applicable domain requires them. They must not be forced into human-only semantics merely because implementation is easier that way.

Participation may be:

`DIRECT AGENCY | REPRESENTED PARTICIPATION | STEWARDSHIP | GUARDIANSHIP | WELFARE / CONDITION`

This allows the architecture to support more-than-human reality without pretending all participants possess the same form of agency or consent.

## 17. Design Invariants

1. Universal participation does not imply universal equivalence.
2. Identity does not imply authority.
3. Intelligence does not imply legitimacy.
4. Capability does not imply permission.
5. Representation does not imply identity with the represented thing.
6. Connectivity does not imply consent.
7. Ownership does not imply unrestricted authority.
8. Observation does not imply truth.
9. Prediction does not imply fact.
10. Autonomy does not imply sovereignty.
11. Shared substrate does not imply shared authority.
12. Not everything that can be governed is owned.
13. Physical and digital are manifestation domains, not separate constitutional universes.
14. Human, AI, robot, animal, biological and institutional participants retain semantics appropriate to their nature.
15. Future or unknown participant types must be representable without redesigning the universal substrate.
16. Every consequential action must remain attributable and auditable to the extent applicable.
17. OMNI is a mode of omnidirectional ecosystem movement, not a privileged architectural layer.
18. OMNII is the canonical architecture/repository; OMNI is not the architecture.
19. Constitutional capability may evolve, but authority remains explicit.
20. No product, agent, runtime component or implementation detail may silently become an alternate constitutional authority.

## 18. Architectural Outcome

OMNII therefore provides one common constitutional substrate for a reality containing different kinds of participants without collapsing their differences.

The architecture can compose:

`humans + animals + biology + AI + robots + machines + software + hardware + organizations + institutions + territories + environments + resources + ecosystems + future forms`

through common identity, relationship, context, knowledge, value, authority, capability, resource, execution, state, evidence, Pulse and Becoming semantics.

> **Everything can participate. Nothing is assumed identical. Every movement has context. Every capability has boundaries. Every consequential change produces state and evidence. Every representation remains distinguishable from what it represents. Every authority is explicit. Every participant retains the semantics appropriate to its nature.**

## 19. Highest-Level Definition

> **OMNII is the canonical ecosystem architecture and repository for composing reality across physical, digital, biological, social and institutional domains. It provides a universal constitutional substrate through which different kinds of participants can be identified, related, represented, observed, governed, coordinated, acted upon, measured and evolved without being reduced to the same kind of thing. OMNI is the omnidirectional mode through which that ecosystem can move.**
