# Carbon Actual — Missing Concepts & Architecture Hardening

**Status:** Canonical architecture extension

## 1. Foundational correction

Carbon Actual must not use **physical vs digital** as a primary architectural split.

The universal framing is:

**Human + Intelligence + Hardware + Software + Institutions + Resources + Environments + Unknown/Future Participants**.

Hardware and software are implementation forms. Humans and AI are participating agents/entities. A capability may be implemented through any combination of hardware, software, human action and intelligence.

NIST's cyber-physical work reinforces that modern systems are co-engineered across physical and computational components and that timing, dependability, security, privacy and data interoperability need to be treated as first-order design concerns. Carbon Actual therefore models these as cross-cutting properties rather than separate worlds.

## 2. Concepts that must be first-class in the universal model

### Intent
What a Being/entity is trying to accomplish.

### Goal / Outcome
The measurable or qualitative result sought by an activity, Team or Product Branch.

### Context
The circumstances under which a capability, decision or relationship is valid.

Context includes role, location, time, jurisdiction, organization, environment, resource state and relevant constraints.

### State
The current condition of an object, capability, relationship, Team, contract, resource or environment.

### Capacity
How much of a capability/resource is currently available.

### Availability
When and under what conditions a capability/resource can be used.

### Constraint
What limits an action, capability, resource or relationship.

### Dependency
What must exist or remain available for an outcome to succeed.

### Requirement
What must be true for a Product, Workflow, Team, Contract or activity to be valid.

### Evidence
What supports a claim, decision, credential, measurement or outcome.

### Trust
A context-sensitive evaluation derived from identity, authority, provenance, providence, behavior, evidence, reputation, security posture and policy—not a permanent binary label.

### Reputation
Observed historical performance/relationship signal, distinct from identity and certification.

### Quality
How well an output satisfies a defined specification or outcome.

### Reliability
Probability/observed ability to continue satisfying a capability contract under stated conditions.

### Resilience
Ability to absorb disruption, degrade safely, recover and continue.

### Safety
Protection against unacceptable harm or unsafe system behavior.

### Risk
Potential negative consequence, uncertainty and exposure.

### Opportunity
A contextually suitable path for value creation, capability growth, participation or Becoming.

### Incentive
A mechanism that changes the attractiveness of an action; incentives must not create hidden coercion or perverse system behavior.

### Obligation
A responsibility or required action linked to authority, contract, governance or economic state.

### Commitment
An explicit promise or agreed future action, with scope and lifecycle.

### Agency
The capacity of a participant to initiate or meaningfully control action within its authority.

### Accountability
The ability to identify responsibility for a decision/action and inspect the evidence and authority behind it.

### Consent
Human or authorized permission appropriate to the action and context; distinct from authentication and identity.

### Delegation
Scoped transfer of authority, capability or responsibility without transferring identity.

### Custody
Who/what currently holds, operates, stores or safeguards something, distinct from ownership.

### Ownership
The rights/responsibilities associated with an asset, resource, data object, intellectual work or other eligible object.

### Stewardship
Responsible care for an object/resource even where ownership is absent or shared.

### Interoperability
Ability for independently developed systems to exchange meaning and operate together. This includes technical, semantic, operational, organizational and cognitive interoperability.

Research on cyber-physical systems identifies interoperability, timing, dependability, security, privacy and semantic context as foundational concerns rather than optional features. Human-AI teaming research additionally highlights dynamic work allocation and human-centric collaboration as important for real systems.

### Cognitive interoperability
Ability of humans, intelligences, agents and systems to understand enough of one another's intent, capability, constraints and outputs to collaborate safely and effectively.

### Semantic interoperability
Shared meaning of objects, events, units, capabilities and relationships.

### Temporal interoperability
Ability to coordinate actions and state across time, deadlines, clocks, sequencing and synchronization.

### Spatial interoperability
Ability to reason about location, proximity, distance, direction, movement and physical constraints where relevant.

### Economic interoperability
Ability to express value, payment, rights, obligations and settlement across systems.

### Governance interoperability
Ability to preserve policy, authority, accountability and regulatory boundaries when capabilities cross organizational boundaries.

### Interface
The declared way a person, agent, hardware component, software system or organization interacts with a capability.

### Protocol
A machine/human-readable agreement governing interaction.

### Contract
The formal semantic/operational commitment between parties or components.

### Version
Which contract/model implementation is being used.

### Compatibility
Whether two versions/components can safely work together.

### Migration
How state and meaning move from one version/provider to another.

### Portability
Ability to move identity, data, capability, configuration or value representations between providers without unnecessary loss.

### Substitution
Replacing one implementation with another while preserving the capability contract.

### Fallback
A predefined alternate path when a primary capability fails or becomes unsuitable.

### Escalation
Movement from autonomous/automatic handling to higher-authority or human intervention when thresholds are crossed.

### Exception
A condition in which normal workflow cannot safely continue.

### Compensation
An action that restores or counterbalances effects after failed/partial activity.

### Simulation
Testing a planned action or system under hypothetical conditions before real deployment.

### Experiment
A controlled activity designed to learn whether a hypothesis or capability works.

### Learning
Updating capability, policy, model or curation based on evidence without rewriting historical facts.

### Memory
Persistent context available to an entity/intelligence under explicit scope and privacy rules.

### Reflection
Ability of a human, intelligence, Team or organization to inspect its own state and improve.

### Evaluation
A structured assessment of quality, capability, risk, cost, performance or suitability.

### Benchmark
A repeatable comparison methodology.

### Optimization
Selecting changes that improve a defined objective under constraints.

### Adaptation
Changing behavior/configuration in response to state, evidence or context.

### Governance feedback
The loop by which outcomes change policy, controls and institutional behavior.

### Resource provenance/providence
A resource must preserve both where it came from/evidence lineage and the context/source of provision/stewardship.

## 3. Human + AI must be treated as a collaboration system

The architecture must not reduce the human to a user and AI to a tool.

A HASH may consist of:

```text
HUMAN
  ↕
INTELLIGENCE
  ↕
CAPABILITY
  ↕
MEMORY / CONTEXT
  ↕
TOOLS / AGENTS
  ↕
HARDWARE / SOFTWARE
  ↕
ENVIRONMENT
```

Human and intelligence retain distinguishable identity, capabilities and authority while forming a governed partnership.

Research on human-AI and cyber-physical human systems supports modeling human capabilities, interfaces and identities explicitly rather than treating humans as passive external users.

## 4. Hardware + software must be the implementation model

A capability may be implemented by:

- human action
- software
- hardware
- firmware
- AI/model
- agent
- machine
- robot
- combination of the above

The architecture must therefore avoid separate primary ontologies for “physical” and “digital.”

Example:

**Vehicle capability** may comprise software + firmware + sensors + motors + battery + network + human operator + AI + maintenance service.

It is one capability composition.

## 5. Missing cross-cutting system properties

Every capability/product should be evaluable across:

- functionality
- quality
- reliability
- availability
- latency
- cost
- energy/resource efficiency
- safety
- security
- privacy
- resilience
- maintainability
- portability
- interoperability
- accessibility
- observability
- explainability where relevant
- auditability
- compliance
- sustainability
- reversibility
- recoverability

These are **properties of capabilities**, not separate product silos.

### Economic loop

### Security loop

`Observation → Detection → Classification → Containment → Recovery → Learning`

### Knowledge loop

`Source → Evidence → Knowledge → Use → Result → New evidence`

### Human loop

`Human intent → Intelligence augmentation → Action → Experience → Reflection → Becoming`

### Organizational loop

`Goal → Capability map → Gap → Curate → Team → Execution → KPI → Reconfigure`

### Maintenance loop

`Use → Condition → Prediction → Maintenance → Repair/Replace → Verification → Return to service`

### Integration loop

`Discover → Evaluate → Adapt → Integrate → Observe → Replace/Substitute → Learn`

### Product loop

`Need → Blueprint → Composer → Swarms → Team → Workflow → Deployment → Pulse → Value → Product evolution`

## 7. New principle: capability is larger than application

A capability is not synonymous with an application, vendor, department or product. Multiple providers may implement one capability. One provider may implement many capabilities.

This is why Swarms and Teams are necessary.

## 8. New principle: context determines suitability

No provider is globally “best.” A capability may be best for a specific context because of privacy, price, latency, availability, regulation, hardware constraints, language, location, reliability, openness or human requirements.

ABBA and the Curation Engine should therefore optimize **fitness to context**, not generic ranking.

## 9. New principle: preserve uncertainty

Unknown, disputed and incomplete information are valid states. The system must not force premature certainty. Unknown values must remain visible and may become future evidence opportunities.

## 10. New principle: boundaries are first-class

Every cross-entity action needs clear boundaries for:

- identity
- authority
- data
- value
- responsibility
- time
- jurisdiction
- safety
- physical/resource constraints

This prevents integration from becoming uncontrolled coupling.

## 11. Architecture rule

The canonical Carbon Actual model is:

**Entity + Identity + Intent + Context + Capability + Capacity + Constraint + Relationship + Activity + Evidence + Pulse + Value + Authority + Provenance + Providence + State + Lifecycle**

This is a more complete universal grammar than an application-centric model while remaining compact enough to compose into any branch.
