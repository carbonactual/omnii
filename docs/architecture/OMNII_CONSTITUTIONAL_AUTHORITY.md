# OMNII Constitutional Authority

**Status:** Constitutional Doctrine  
**Layer:** Coordination / Legitimation  
**Canonical authority:** OMNII Canonical Architecture

## 1. Purpose

OMNII Constitutional Authority defines the legitimate capacity of an entity, role, institution, agent, or system to act, decide, authorize, delegate, constrain, or represent within a defined constitutional scope.

Authority is not synonymous with power, control, capability, ownership, or intelligence.

## 2. Core Definition

> **Authority is constitutionally recognized capacity to produce, approve, direct, delegate, constrain, or represent action within defined scope, subject to legitimate source, duration, conditions, and accountability.**

## 3. Authority Invariants

### 3.1 Source Invariant

Every material authority claim must have an identifiable source or legitimate basis.

### 3.2 Scope Invariant

Authority must have an express or inferable constitutional scope. Authority in one domain does not automatically extend to another.

### 3.3 Subject Invariant

The holder of authority must be identifiable.

### 3.4 Target Invariant

Where authority governs another entity, resource, process, or domain, the affected target must be identifiable.

### 3.5 Temporal Invariant

Authority may be time-bounded, conditional, suspended, revoked, or superseded. Its effective period must remain representable.

### 3.6 Delegation Invariant

Delegated authority cannot exceed the legitimate scope of the authority from which it derives.

### 3.7 Accountability Invariant

Material authority actions must remain attributable and reviewable.

### 3.8 Revocation Invariant

The system must be capable of representing withdrawal, suspension, expiration, or invalidation of authority.

### 3.9 Non-Inference Invariant

Physical presence, technical capability, possession of information, ownership of an implementation, or intelligence does not by itself establish constitutional authority.

## 4. Authority Sources

Authority may arise from:

- constitutional mandate;
- law or jurisdiction;
- institutional charter;
- delegated role;
- contract;
- membership rules;
- valid consent;
- stewardship arrangement;
- fiduciary or representative relationship;
- other explicitly recognized constitutional mechanisms.

The system must preserve the distinction between the source of authority and the entity exercising it.

## 5. Authority Lifecycle

```text
Proposed
  ↓
Granted / Recognized
  ↓
Active
  ↓
Delegated / Modified / Suspended
  ↓
Expired / Revoked / Superseded
```

Each transition should be attributable and temporally coherent.

## 6. Delegation

Delegation creates a relationship between a delegator and delegate.

```text
Principal Authority
      ↓ bounded delegation
Delegate
      ↓ bounded action
Target / Domain
```

Delegation must preserve:

- source;
- scope;
- conditions;
- duration;
- accountability;
- revocation path.

A delegate cannot constitutionally expand its authority merely by exercising it.

## 7. Authority and Governance

Governance coordinates collective action. Authority legitimates defined capacity to act within that coordination.

```text
Governance = coordination
Authority  = legitimate capacity
```

Governance without legitimate authority can become arbitrary control. Authority without governance can become disconnected power.

## 8. Authority and Consent

Consent may establish or limit authority in some relationships, but consent is not the universal source of all authority. The constitutional source must remain explicit.

Where consent is required, authority cannot be used to bypass the applicable consent boundary.

## 9. Authority and Territory

Territorial jurisdiction can be a source of authority, but geographic presence alone is not authority.

Territorial authority must remain distinguishable from:

- location;
- ownership;
- physical control;
- service coverage.

## 10. Authority and ABBA

ABBA may exercise only authority explicitly delegated or constitutionally granted to the relevant ABBA instance or workflow.

ABBA's intelligence does not create authority.

```text
Intelligence ≠ Authority
Capability  ≠ Authority
Observation ≠ Authority
```

Automated action must remain traceable to its authority basis.

## 11. Authority and OMNI

OMNI is the omnidirectional ecosystem movement state. Authority constrains or legitimates particular actions within the ecosystem; it does not redefine OMNI as an authority system.

```text
OMNII = canonical architecture
OMNI  = omnidirectional movement state
Authority = legitimate bounded capacity
```

## 12. Failure Conditions

Constitutional authority failure includes:

- authority without source;
- authority exceeding scope;
- delegation exceeding source authority;
- action after revocation or expiration;
- untraceable material decisions;
- authority inferred from technical capability;
- authority inferred from location alone;
- automated agents acting without a valid authority path;
- authority being treated as ownership of the ecosystem.

## 13. Constitutional Dependencies

Authority depends on:

- Governance
- Relationship
- Continuity
- Territory where territorial authority applies
- Consent where consent is required

Authority provides a foundation for:

- Consent
- Being
- Becoming
- Civilization

## 14. Final Principle

> **Legitimate authority is power made bounded, attributable, contextual, and accountable by constitutional structure.**
