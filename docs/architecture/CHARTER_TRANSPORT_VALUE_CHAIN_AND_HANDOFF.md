# CHARTER — Transport Value Chain & Institutional Composition

**Status:** Canonical Charter architecture supplement
**Domain:** Universal transport / movement
**Boundary:** IO owns value movement; Charter owns transport/movement context and orchestration.

## Purpose

Charter models transport as a complete ecosystem rather than a booking application. The transport graph includes movement capabilities, people, organizations, institutions, authorities, infrastructure, equipment, services, information, availability, condition, lifecycle, journeys and the workflows that connect them.

Charter is deliberately broad. It can compose any canonical capability required to execute movement while preserving canonical ownership elsewhere in OMNII.

## Economic boundary

Charter can represent transport requirements, economic relationships as references, and workflow triggers, but **value movement belongs to IO**. Charter must not create a parallel money, settlement, payment, pricing or economic primitive.

## Security and sensitive movement

Charter can represent authorized security, emergency, government and protective movement requirements at the appropriate abstraction level. Sensitive operational details, restricted capabilities and jurisdiction-specific permissions must remain subject to canonical authority, policy and evidence controls.

## Future and unknown movement

The architecture is open to new terrestrial, marine, aerial, autonomous, orbital, extraterrestrial or otherwise novel movement capabilities. Representation does not imply factual existence. Unknown, reported, hypothetical, simulated and verified states must remain distinguishable.

## Constitutional rule

> **Charter is the transport/movement capability and orchestration domain. It is broad by composition, not by duplication. Every capability has a canonical source; Charter composes it through relationships, workflows and movement context.**
