# Carbon Actual Governance, Policy, Decision & Dispute Contract

**Status:** Canonical kernel contract

## Purpose

Governance defines how decisions are made, constrained, reviewed, delegated, appealed and enforced across humans, intelligences, organizations, infrastructure and future participant classes.

## Governance hierarchy

```text
BEING
 ↓
CONSTITUTION
 ↓
PRINCIPLES
 ↓
LAWS
 ↓
POLICIES
 ↓
RULES
 ↓
WORKFLOWS
 ↓
DECISIONS
 ↓
ACTIONS
```

Higher layers constrain lower layers.

## Constitutional principles

The ecosystem should preserve:

- continuity
- curation
- union
- stewardship
- accountability
- explainability
- interoperability
- integration
- proportionality
- human dignity
- privacy
- resilience
- no-waste recovery

## Authority

Authority is the recognized ability to make, approve, reject, delegate or enforce decisions within a defined scope.

Authority must be explicit, attributable and bounded.

## Decision

A Decision is a governed selection among alternatives.

Every material decision should preserve:

- decision maker
- authority basis
- alternatives considered
- evidence used
- rationale
- affected parties
- time
- resulting action

## Policy

A Policy defines how decisions should be made under recurring conditions.

Policies should be versioned and auditable.

## Rules

Rules are operational constraints derived from policy.

Rules may be automated, human-enforced or hybrid.

## Delegation

Authority may be delegated without transferring identity.

Delegation must remain traceable.

## Appeals

Material decisions should support appeal, review or escalation when appropriate.

The system should avoid irreversible governance outcomes where review is possible and justified.

## Disputes

A dispute exists when participants disagree regarding facts, rights, obligations, value, responsibility, outcomes or authority.

Disputes are first-class objects.

## Dispute lifecycle

```text
RAISED
 ↓
ACKNOWLEDGED
 ↓
EVIDENCE COLLECTION
 ↓
REVIEW
 ↓
RESOLUTION
 ↓
SETTLEMENT
 ↓
LEARNING
```

## Governance of intelligences

No intelligence receives unlimited authority.

Intelligences operate within explicit authority boundaries, policy constraints and review mechanisms.

## Human-first principle

Where a capable human can appropriately perform an action, the ecosystem should prefer enabling that human rather than unnecessarily replacing them.

## Future participant principle

Governance must be extensible to new participant classes, including future machine, synthetic, autonomous or currently unknown participant types.

## Universal rule

Every product branch inherits the same governance grammar instead of creating isolated governance systems.
