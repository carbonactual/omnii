# OMNII Constitutional Economics v1.0

Status: Constitutional Doctrine

Purpose: Define the constitutional behavior of value, contribution, return, stewardship, exchange, obligation, reciprocity, scarcity, abundance and continuity across all forms of participation.

## Foundational Axioms

1. Participation precedes economics.
2. Value precedes money.
3. Contribution precedes return.
4. Stewardship creates value.
5. Trust is economic infrastructure.
6. Relationships are economic pathways.
7. Rights and obligations remain coupled.
8. Continuity is a primary economic objective.

## Value Doctrine

Value may be expressed through time, effort, knowledge, trust, relationships, energy, resources, intelligence, stewardship, infrastructure, preservation and monetary exchange.

Money is a representation of value, not value itself.

## Contribution Doctrine

Every participant contributes directly or indirectly to ecosystem state.

Contribution may be active, passive, immediate, delayed, material or immaterial.

## Return Doctrine

Return is contextual and may be economic, social, biological, informational, institutional or reputational.

## Trust Economics

Trust reduces friction, increases coordination and expands productive capacity.

## Stewardship Economics

Preservation, maintenance, repair and continuity are productive economic activities.

## Scarcity and Abundance

Scarcity is contextual insufficiency.

Abundance is contextual sufficiency.

Neither is universally fixed.

## Reciprocity Doctrine

Value may return directly, indirectly or across generations.

## Continuity Economics

The highest constitutional objective is sustainable continuity rather than isolated extraction.

## Constitutional Economic Invariants

- Participation precedes economics.
- Value is contextual.
- Extraction without contribution weakens ecosystems.
- Preservation is productive.
- Future participants matter.
- Economic legitimacy requires evidence.
- Continuity matters.
