# TIP — Universal & Frontier Market Completeness

**Ecosystem:** Carbon Actual / OMNII  
**Product:** TIP — Trade, Investment & Value Markets  
**Status:** CANONICAL EXTENSION — approved  
**Purpose:** Stress-test and expand TIP against historical, current, emerging, prospective, futuristic, machine-native, biological, environmental, space/cislunar and explicitly unknown/non-human economic scenarios without creating a second economic ontology.

---

## 1. Why this extension exists

A universal market architecture cannot be validated only against the markets that are common today.

TIP must be able to recognize:

```text
HISTORICAL
CURRENT
EMERGING
PROSPECTIVE
EXPERIMENTAL
SPECULATIVE
UNKNOWN
```

without treating those maturity labels as legal permission, market status, ownership, personhood or factual certainty.

The test is simple:

> **Can a market or economic relationship be represented by composing existing TIP objects, rights/claims, market forms, operating objects, evidence, authority, settlement and Value/Pulse feedback rather than inventing a new economic ontology?**

If yes, the concept is a facet or instance. If no, TIP adds the missing primitive once and reuses it everywhere.

---

## 2. Universal market completeness principle

TIP must separate at least eight dimensions:

```text
WHAT EXISTS?
WHO / WHAT PARTICIPATES?
WHAT RELATIONSHIP EXISTS?
WHAT IS BEING EXCHANGED / ALLOCATED?
WHEN / WHERE DOES IT HAPPEN?
HOW ARE TERMS FORMED?
HOW IS PERFORMANCE / SETTLEMENT VERIFIED?
WHAT ECONOMIC OUTCOME OCCURRED?
```

These dimensions map to the existing TIP stack:

```text
OBJECT
 ↓
RELATION / RIGHT / CLAIM
 ↓
MARKET FORM
 ↓
OPERATING LIFECYCLE
 ↓
EVIDENCE / MEASUREMENT
 ↓
VALUE / PULSE
```

No market family is allowed to bypass the stack.

## 3. Historical economic forms

Historical systems are not obsolete data. They are evidence of reusable market primitives.

### 3.1 Barter / direct exchange

```text
GOOD ↔ GOOD
SERVICE ↔ GOOD
SERVICE ↔ SERVICE
```

Primitive requirements:

```text
MULTI-UNIT EXCHANGE
RELATIVE VALUATION
COUNTERPARTY MATCH
DELIVERY
CONDITION
```

### 3.2 Gift / reciprocity

Exchange may occur without an immediate contractual price.

```text
GIFT
RECIPROCAL GIFT
SOCIAL OBLIGATION
FUTURE RECIPROCITY
```

Historical research on economic exchange distinguishes reciprocity and redistribution from market exchange, showing that economic systems can organize through non-price relationships as well as markets. citeturn659858search13turn835382search4

TIP representation:

```text
OFFER = GIFT
PRICE = NONE
OBLIGATION = OPTIONAL / SOCIAL / CONTRACTUAL
RECIPROCITY = RELATIONSHIP
```

### 3.3 Redistribution

```text
COLLECTION
 ↓
POOL
 ↓
ALLOCATION
 ↓
RECIPIENTS
```

Examples include public allocation, taxation-and-spending systems, institutional distributions and pooled resources.

### 3.4 Tribute / levy / customary obligation

These should be represented as:

```text
OBLIGATION
AUTHORITY BASIS
ASSESSMENT
COLLECTION
ALLOCATION
```

not automatically as voluntary market purchases.

### 3.5 Fairs / bazaars / periodic markets

```text
MARKET WINDOW
TEMPORARY VENUE
MULTIPLE COUNTERPARTIES
LOCAL PRICE FORMATION
BARGAINING
PHYSICAL DELIVERY
```

The historical existence of market institutions demonstrates that venue, time window and discovery mechanism can be distinct from the underlying objects.

### 3.6 Merchant credit / bills of exchange

Bills of exchange connected cross-border trade, credit and settlement long before modern electronic markets. Research on late-medieval and early-modern Europe identifies cashless settlement, merchant-bank credit, negotiability and double-entry bookkeeping as important elements in their development. citeturn659858search4turn659858search9

TIP therefore treats:

```text
CLAIM
NEGOTIABLE CLAIM
TRADE FINANCE
CROSS-BORDER SETTLEMENT
AGENCY
LEDGER
```

as reusable primitives.

### 3.7 Warehouse / receipt / commodity representation

A physical stock may be represented by evidence or a transferable claim without moving the physical commodity.

```text
UNDERLYING INVENTORY
 ↓
WAREHOUSE / CUSTODY
 ↓
CERTIFICATE / RECEIPT
 ↓
CLAIM
 ↓
TRANSFER / FINANCE / COLLATERAL
```

### 3.8 Early clearing and netting

TIP treats clearing/netting as reusable infrastructure, not merely a feature of modern derivatives markets.

## 10. Extra-terrestrial resource market boundary

TIP may represent future economic objects beyond Earth without making unsupported claims about current legal ownership or market availability.

Possible objects:


Current NASA activity explicitly discusses lunar resource-seeking and ISRU technologies, including water ice, minerals, hydrogen and helium-3, while treating these within ongoing exploration and development programs. citeturn835382search8



## 11. Unknown / hypothetical non-human actors

“Aliens” are treated as a **boundary case**, not a factual assertion.

TIP should reserve explicit classes for unknown participants or objects discovered through credible evidence:

```text
UNKNOWN_NON_HUMAN_ACTOR
UNKNOWN_NON_HUMAN_INTELLIGENCE
EXTRATERRESTRIAL_OBJECT
EXTRATERRESTRIAL_BIOLOGICAL_SAMPLE
EXTRATERRESTRIAL_DATA
UNKNOWN_ORIGIN_ARTIFACT
```

Required fields:

```yaml
identity_confidence:
evidence_source:
provenance:
observation_time:
observation_location:
verification_status:
human_interpreter:
authority_basis:
jurisdiction:
contact_status:
communication_protocol:
rights_status:
contamination_risk:
security_status:
```

Canonical rule:

> **Unknown is an epistemic state, not ownership, personhood, citizenship, market eligibility or consent.**

No economic transaction with a hypothetical non-human actor may be assumed to exist. Activation would require credible evidence, verified identity/communication as applicable, authority, safety review and whatever legal/international framework governs the situation.

## 18. Market stress-test matrix

Every newly discovered market candidate should answer:

| Dimension | Canonical question |
|---|---|
| Object | What actually exists? |
| Participant | Who/what acts? |
| Authority | Who may authorize the action? |
| Right | What entitlement is involved? |
| Claim | What economic claim exists? |
| Market form | How are terms formed? |
| Time | When is availability/exchange valid? |
| Space | Where does it apply? |
| Contract | What obligations bind parties? |
| Evidence | What proves the relevant fact? |
| Measurement | How is quantity/quality/outcome measured? |
| Risk | What can fail or cause harm? |
| Settlement | How is value discharged? |
| Accounting | What economic event is recognized? |
| Lifecycle | What happens next? |
| Pulse | What feedback indicates usefulness? |

If a proposed market cannot answer these questions, it is not yet sufficiently specified for activation.

## 19. What “universal” means — and does not mean

Universal does **not** mean:

```text
EVERYTHING IS LEGAL
EVERYTHING IS FOR SALE
EVERYTHING IS TOKENIZABLE
EVERYTHING HAS A PRICE
EVERYTHING HAS PRIVATE OWNERSHIP
EVERYTHING CAN BE AUTOMATED
```

Universal means:

```text
THE ARCHITECTURE CAN REPRESENT THE ECONOMIC RELATIONSHIP
AND THEN APPLY THE CORRECT AUTHORITY, LAW, ETHICS, SAFETY,
EVIDENCE, RISK AND SETTLEMENT RULES.
```

---

## 20. Canonical frontier invariant

> **TIP must be capable of representing economic systems humans have used, are using, are beginning to build, may plausibly build, or may one day encounter, while preserving the distinction between evidence and inference, representation and reality, capability and authority, possibility and fact, and marketability and legality.**

This keeps TIP open-ended without turning speculation into ontology or permission.
