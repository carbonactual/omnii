# OMNII Constitutional Consent

**Status:** Constitutional Doctrine  
**Layer:** Coordination / Participation  
**Canonical authority:** OMNII Canonical Architecture

## 1. Purpose

OMNII Constitutional Consent defines the constitutional boundaries of legitimate participation, agreement, authorization, refusal, withdrawal, and representation where consent is required or materially relevant.

Consent is a specific constitutional relationship. It must not be confused with silence, presence, compliance, awareness, or mere interaction.

## 2. Core Definition

> **Consent is a constitutionally meaningful expression of agreement or authorization by an identifiable participant, within defined scope and conditions, where that participant has legitimate capacity to provide it.**

## 3. Consent Invariants

### 3.1 Capacity Invariant

The consenting participant must have the relevant capacity or recognized representation to provide consent.

### 3.2 Scope Invariant

Consent applies only to the matter, action, relationship, or domain for which it was given.

### 3.3 Information Invariant

Where informed consent is required, relevant information necessary for meaningful agreement must be available to the participant.

### 3.4 Voluntariness Invariant

Consent must remain distinguishable from coercion, deception, or illegitimate pressure.

### 3.5 Specificity Invariant

Consent for one purpose must not silently become consent for unrelated purposes.

### 3.6 Temporal Invariant

Consent may be time-bounded, conditional, withdrawn, renewed, or superseded.

### 3.7 Provenance Invariant

Material consent must remain attributable to its source and formation context.

### 3.8 Withdrawal Invariant

Where withdrawal is constitutionally available, the system must represent it and its effective time and scope.

### 3.9 Non-Inference Invariant

Presence, participation, silence, usage, or failure to object does not automatically constitute consent.

## 4. Consent Forms

Consent may include:

- explicit agreement;
- authorization;
- opt-in participation;
- delegated consent;
- representative consent where legitimately recognized;
- continuing consent where explicitly established and revocable;
- conditional consent.

Different domains may impose different requirements.

## 5. Consent Lifecycle

```text
Requested
   ↓
Informed
   ↓
Granted / Refused
   ↓
Active
   ↓
Modified / Withdrawn / Expired
```

A system must not represent withdrawn consent as active merely because an earlier consent record exists.

## 6. Consent and Authority

Consent and authority are related but distinct.

```text
Authority = legitimate capacity to act
Consent   = legitimate participation/agreement where required
```

Consent may grant or constrain authority in some relationships. Authority may also arise from sources other than consent.

## 7. Consent and Governance

Governance must preserve participation boundaries established by applicable consent.

Collective governance does not automatically eliminate individual consent requirements.

## 8. Consent and Relationship

Consent can create, modify, or terminate certain relationships. The underlying relationship and the consent event must remain distinguishable.

## 9. Consent and ABBA

ABBA may request, record, verify, route, and enforce consent-dependent workflows where constitutionally authorized.

ABBA must not infer consent from silence or manufacture consent through automation.

Automated actions must respect consent state and effective dates.

## 10. Consent and Human Becoming

Consent is central to human agency because becoming includes choices about participation, relationships, opportunities, services, and identity.

The ecosystem must preserve the person's ability to distinguish what they have agreed to from what merely happened around them.

## 11. Consent and OMNI

OMNI is the omnidirectional ecosystem movement state. Consent establishes participation boundaries where applicable; it does not redefine OMNI as a consent system, governance layer, authority layer, platform, or runtime.

```text
OMNII = canonical architecture
OMNI  = omnidirectional movement state
Consent = bounded legitimate participation
```

## 12. Failure Conditions

Constitutional consent failure includes:

- fabricated consent;
- consent inferred from silence;
- consent used beyond scope;
- hidden material changes after consent;
- inability to withdraw where withdrawal is required;
- unrecognized representative authority;
- consent recorded without provenance;
- automation acting after withdrawal;
- collective governance being used to erase legitimate individual consent boundaries.

## 13. Constitutional Dependencies

Consent depends on:

- Authority
- Relationship
- Governance
- Continuity
- Semantics

Consent provides a foundation for:

- Being
- Becoming
- Civilization

## 14. Final Principle

> **Consent makes participation legible without turning participation into ownership or authority.**
