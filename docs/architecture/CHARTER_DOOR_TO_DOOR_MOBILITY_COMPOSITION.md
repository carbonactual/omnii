# CHARTER — Door-to-Door Integrated Mobility Composition

**Status:** Canonical Charter composition supplement  
**Architecture position:** Transport/movement composition over existing OMNII primitives.  
**Economic boundary:** Value movement remains in IO.

## Purpose

Charter is not limited to a single transport service or vehicle category. It composes the complete movement journey from origin to destination, including multimodal handoffs and the lawful participation of government, public, private, commercial, community and individual mobility resources.

The product principle is:

> **Door to door, through whatever combination of movement capabilities is required.**

Charter may therefore coordinate a journey using private vehicles, public transport, government transport infrastructure, commercial operators, individual providers, shared mobility, walking, lifts, escalators, boats, aircraft or future movement systems.

## Integrated movement chain

```text
ORIGIN
  ↓
ACCESS / PICKUP
  ↓
LOCAL MOVEMENT
  ↓
TRANSFER
  ↓
PRIMARY TRANSPORT
  ↓
INTERCHANGE
  ↓
SECONDARY TRANSPORT
  ↓
LAST MILE
  ↓
DESTINATION
```

A journey may contain any number of valid movement legs and handoffs.

## Resource composition

Charter can compose movement resources from:

- government and public-sector assets;
- public infrastructure;
- private vehicles and vessels;
- commercial operators;
- individual owners/providers;
- community transport;
- fleets;
- shared mobility;
- emergency and humanitarian resources where authorized;
- specialized operators and equipment;
- future and emerging transport systems.

The existence of a resource in a Charter workflow does **not** transfer ownership or authority to Charter.

## Door-to-door orchestration

A request can be represented as:

`INTENT → ORIGIN → DESTINATION → TIME → REQUIREMENTS → DISCOVER → COMPOSE LEGS → VERIFY CAPABILITY → CHECK AVAILABILITY → CHECK AUTHORITY → ASSIGN/RESERVE → EXECUTE → MONITOR → HANDLE EXCEPTIONS → HANDOFF → ARRIVE → RECORD`

Requirements may include accessibility, passenger needs, cargo requirements, vehicle characteristics, environmental constraints, specialist personnel, timing, route constraints and safety requirements.

## Government integration

Charter can integrate government/public assets and infrastructure where lawful interfaces, permissions and operating responsibilities exist. Examples include public transit, roads, bridges, stations, terminals, emergency infrastructure and other government-controlled movement resources.

Government authority remains represented through OMNII's canonical authority and governance semantics. Charter does not manufacture permissions or override jurisdiction.

## Private and individual integration

Charter can compose privately owned or individually operated resources when their capabilities, availability, identity, authority and applicable requirements are established. This includes privately owned vehicles, boats, aircraft, specialist equipment and human-operated services.

Individual participation does not require creation of a separate transport primitive.

## Opportunity surface

The integrated movement model creates room for products and workflows across the movement value chain: dispatch, fleet operations, shared movement, routing, transfers, maintenance, recovery, infrastructure utilization, specialist labor, transport education, accessibility, logistics, tourism, pilgrimage, events, emergency response and emerging mobility.

These opportunities are **compositions over Charter and other canonical ecosystem capabilities**, not permission to create duplicate primitives.

## Future extensibility

Charter remains open to new movement modes, autonomous systems, experimental systems, extraterrestrial systems and other future capabilities. New modes enter through canonical object, capability, authority, lifecycle, evidence and relationship semantics rather than requiring a new constitutional primitive.

## Source-of-truth rule

Before adding any Charter capability:

1. Identify the canonical source of truth.
2. Reuse the existing primitive/capability.
3. Add only the movement relationship or context needed by Charter.
4. Preserve identity, authority, provenance, lifecycle, evidence and state.
5. Route value movement to **IO**.
6. Do not create duplicate economic, identity, person, vehicle, property, authority, registry or workflow primitives.

## Boundary

Charter owns the **transport/movement context and orchestration**. It does not become the canonical source for every object it moves.

A mansion can remain a canonical property/place object while being a Charter destination. A person remains a canonical human object while acting as a driver. A government station remains a canonical infrastructure/place object while serving as a transport interchange. An economic transaction remains an IO concern.

This enables a broad, capability-rich Charter without architectural overlap.
