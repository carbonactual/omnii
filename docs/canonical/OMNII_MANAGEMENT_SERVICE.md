# OMNII Universal Management Service

**Status:** CANONICAL SERVICE DEFINITION
**Scope:** Global / modular / configurable

## 1. Service Definition

The **Management Service** provides management capability on demand across a subject, objective, project, organization, asset, service, agent, institution, network or ecosystem.

A Management Service is not a person pretending to be an organization. It is a composable set of management capabilities, interfaces, workflows, intelligence, controls and service commitments configured to a declared scope.

## 3. Service Classes

### Personal Management
Time, commitments, tasks, finances, projects, goals, learning, resources, wellbeing-supporting coordination and continuity.

### Household Management
Shared resources, schedules, obligations, maintenance, purchases, services, events, safety and household continuity.

### Project Management
Scope, objectives, tasks, resources, dependencies, schedules, risks, quality, budgets, stakeholders, delivery and closure.

### Program Management
Coordination of related projects and capabilities toward a larger outcome, including dependency, benefit, risk and portfolio alignment.

### Team Management
Objectives, work allocation, collaboration, capacity, performance, capability development, communication and escalation.

### Enterprise Management
Strategy-to-operation coordination across functions, resources, products, services, customers, people, technology, finance, risk and transformation.

### Institutional Management
Mandates, policies, programs, services, registries, staff, assets, public/stakeholder outcomes, accountability and continuity.

### Public / Program Management
Outcome-based coordination of public programs and services, procurement, stakeholders, resources, evidence, compliance and delivery without substituting for lawful public authority.

### Operations Management
Day-to-day throughput, capacity, quality, work, inventory, service levels, incidents, maintenance and operational continuity.

### Asset Management
Lifecycle, location, custody, condition, utilization, maintenance, depreciation, value, risk, replacement and retirement.

### Resource Management
People, machines, money, energy, materials, compute, facilities, time, attention and capacity.

### Service Management
Service design, service delivery, support, incidents, requests, changes, capacity, quality, availability, service relationships and continual improvement.

### Product Management
Intent, user/problem understanding, opportunity, requirements, prioritization, composition, experience, delivery, outcomes and lifecycle.

### Portfolio Management
Prioritization and balancing of multiple investments, products, projects, capabilities or initiatives against shared constraints and outcomes.

### Financial / Value Management
Budgeting, forecasting, allocation, commitments, liquidity needs, value measurement, economic outcomes, leakage and financial-control coordination.

### Risk Management
Risk identification, assessment, prioritization, treatment, monitoring, incident response and resilience.

### Quality Management
Requirements, quality criteria, verification, inspection, defects, corrective action, standards, audits and improvement.

### Compliance Management
Applicable obligations, control mapping, evidence, deadlines, assessments, remediation and regulated handoffs.

### Data / Knowledge Management
Data lifecycle, knowledge organization, access, provenance, lineage, retention, quality, retrieval and use.

### AI / Agent Management
Identity, delegation, permissions, tools, budgets, objectives, context, evaluation, monitoring, incidents, revocation and lifecycle.

### Infrastructure / Facility Management
Physical or digital facilities, capacity, maintenance, utilization, safety, service continuity and lifecycle.

### Supply / Capacity Management
Demand, supply, capacity, sourcing, procurement, inventory, allocation, bottlenecks, substitution and resilience.

### Relationship / Partner Management
Relationships, commitments, service/provider performance, communication, collaboration, trust evidence, disputes and renewal.

### Change / Transformation Management
Design and execution of transitions, stakeholder coordination, readiness, migration, stabilization, benefit realization and learning.

### Crisis / Continuity Management
Preparedness, detection, response, escalation, alternate paths, recovery, stabilization and post-event learning.

### Ecosystem / Network Management
Interdependent actors, shared resources, protocols, corridors, capabilities, opportunities, dependencies, incentives, outcomes and cross-boundary coordination.

## 4. Service Tiers

### Observe
State, data, events, metrics and exceptions.

### Advise
Analysis, options, recommendations and scenarios.

### Coordinate
Tasks, people, agents, resources, communications and dependencies.

### Execute
Authorized actions through the execution fabric.

### Assure
Verification, controls, quality, compliance, risk and evidence.

### Recover
Incident handling, rerouting, rollback, substitution and continuity.

### Improve
Outcome analysis, Pulse, learning, process redesign and capability evolution.

## 5. Management-as-a-Service Modes

The service can be provisioned as:

- self-managed with ecosystem assistance;
- AI-assisted manager;
- managed by a human professional/operator;
- human + agent management team;
- delegated bounded agent management;
- automated policy-bound management;
- federated/shared management across entities.

Service commitments should be defined by measurable outcomes such as:

- response time;
- decision latency;
- availability;
- throughput;
- resolution time;
- quality;
- cost efficiency;
- risk posture;
- resource utilization;
- continuity;
- stakeholder outcomes.

A service-level statement must identify scope, measurement method, evidence source, period and exception conditions.

## 12. Service Safety

Management Services must not silently acquire new authority through automation, integration or delegation.

High-consequence actions require explicit authority and appropriate controls. Professional, legal, financial, medical, regulatory, safety and other restricted decisions must respect applicable jurisdictional boundaries and route to authorized actors when required.

## 13. Service Completion

A Management Service has delivered its contracted outcome when:

```text
scope addressed
+ authority respected
+ declared work completed or exceptioned
+ evidence captured
+ outcome measured
+ resources reconciled
+ value/Pulse recorded
+ next state established
```

## 14. Universal Service Principle

> **Management is a service the ecosystem can provide wherever coordinated direction is required; the service changes its surface and specialization, not its underlying semantics.**
