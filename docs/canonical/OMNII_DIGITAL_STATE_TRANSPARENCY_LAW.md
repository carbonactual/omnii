# OMNII Digital State & Transparency Law

## Status

Canonical architectural law.

## Purpose

The Internet continuously creates, stores, accesses, derives, copies, remembers, expires, shares, caches, indexes, transforms, and deletes digital state around a human. That state MUST be representable through the existing OMNII universal body even when a provider, browser, device, application, agent, or intermediary does not expose it cleanly to the human.

This law does NOT create a new canonical object or registry. It requires existing universal dimensions to be applied to hidden or poorly explained digital state.

## Core rule

No materially relevant digital state may become invisible merely because a provider calls it a cookie, cache, preference, token, history entry, upload, bookmark, telemetry event, local record, inferred profile, browser state, consent record, session, permission, or implementation detail.

Where technically observable or credibly attested, the ecosystem SHOULD represent:

`what exists → why it exists → who created/accesses it → authority → purpose → data involved → dependency → state → history → provenance → retention → sharing → transformation → expiry → revocation → recovery → portability → exit`

## Coverage

The law covers, at minimum:

- Terms, policies, notices, licenses, consent and acknowledgement records.
- Account, session, authentication, authenticator, recovery and identity state.
- Permissions and permission lifetimes, including granted, denied, prompted, expired and revoked states.
- Cookies, local/session storage, IndexedDB, CacheStorage, service-worker state, browser cache and other storage/access technologies.
- Tracking pixels, scripts, tags, link decoration, navigational tracking, device/browser fingerprinting and advertising identifiers.
- Bookmarks, favorites, saved searches, reading lists, downloads and user-curated destinations.
- Uploads, downloads, transformations, previews, transcoding, OCR, malware scans, moderation, indexing and generated derivatives.
- Browsing, search, interaction, purchase, subscription, communication, security, access and agent histories.
- Telemetry, analytics, diagnostics, crash reports, performance records and product instrumentation.
- Notifications, push subscriptions, alarms, reminders, device permissions and background activity.
- Data supplied by the human, observed by the service, generated by the service, derived by the service, inferred by the service, or imported from third parties.
- Access records, sharing events, exports, imports, synchronization and replication.
- Credentials, keys, passkeys, verification methods, federation assertions, delegated capabilities and their rotation/revocation/recovery.
- Profiles, segments, classifications, reputation signals and inferred attributes, including shadow profiles where evidence exists.
- Recommendations, ranking signals, advertisements, targeting and attribution events.
- Data retention, deletion, legal holds, backup retention, cache persistence and residual copies.
- Provider shutdown, account suspension, lockout, migration, switching, portability and final exit.

## Explanation right

Where the user can reasonably benefit from explanation, Desk SHOULD be able to answer:

1. What is this?
2. Why does it exist?
3. Who controls it?
4. Who can access it?
5. Under what authority?
6. What data does it contain or reveal?
7. What was it derived from?
8. What has changed?
9. How long will it persist?
10. What survives deletion?
11. What breaks if it is revoked?
12. Can it be exported with meaning and relationships intact?
13. Can it be corrected, disputed, restricted or deleted?
14. What evidence supports the explanation?

## Deletion is not assumed to be erasure

A delete action MUST NOT be treated as proof that all relevant copies are gone. The system SHOULD distinguish:

`requested deletion → provider accepted → primary deleted → indexes updated → caches expired → replicas processed → backups pending/retained → downstream copies unknown/confirmed → deletion completed/partially completed/disputed`

## Consent is not authority to everything

A user selecting an interface control MUST NOT be interpreted as blanket permission for unrelated capabilities. Consent, agreement, authentication, ownership, account access and authority remain distinct.

## Provider transparency is not required for representation

A provider's terminology or UI is not canonical. Where provider evidence is incomplete, OMNII MAY record the state as observed, inferred, asserted, unknown, disputed or unavailable, while preserving provenance and confidence.

## Standards are adapters

Cookies, Web Storage, CacheStorage, Service Workers, Permissions API, OAuth/OIDC, passkeys/WebAuthn, DIDs, Verifiable Credentials, GPC, DNT, APIs, browser extension APIs, cloud storage and provider-specific mechanisms are implementation or transport mechanisms. None becomes a competing OMNII primitive.

## Required invariant

A human SHOULD be able to understand the important digital state that materially affects their identity, authority, data, money, privacy, security, continuity, portability or exit, even when that state is distributed across providers and technical layers.
