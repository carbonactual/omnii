**Status:** Draft Canonical / Architecture Extension

## Purpose

This document broadens the OMNII economic architecture beyond finance into a complete value system capable of representing the global economy as interacting value, rights, obligations, resources, capabilities, capacities, services, objects, agents, accounts, markets, contracts and settlements.

The system must support conventional, informal, emerging, digital, decentralized, circular, institutional, public, community, cultural and future economic forms while preserving legal, regulatory, ethical, safety and human-authority boundaries.

## 1. Supreme Economic Principle

> Every legitimate value should be discoverable, identifiable, measurable, rights-aware, usable, exchangeable, financeable where appropriate, protectable, settleable, auditable and recoverable.

Value should not be treated as worthless solely because it is small, fragmented, dormant, expiring, non-monetary, unconventional, difficult to price or outside conventional banking categories.

The goal is not forced monetization. The goal is useful utilization, fair exchange, responsible stewardship and maximum legitimate value realization.

## 3. Core Economic Primitives

### Value Object
Any identifiable object, resource, capability, service, entitlement, account, claim, asset, obligation, capacity, time allocation or other economic unit.

### Value Event
A state-changing event involving value, rights, obligations, ownership, custody, use, access, production, exchange, financing, conversion or settlement.

### Right
A legally, contractually or institutionally recognized ability to own, possess, use, access, transfer, receive, redeem, license or enforce.

### Obligation
A duty to pay, deliver, perform, maintain, repay, insure, preserve or otherwise satisfy a condition.

### Agreement
A structured relationship that creates or changes rights, obligations, conditions, milestones and settlement rules.

### Account
A stateful container or relationship for balances, claims, entitlements, obligations or positions.

### Agent
A human, organization, AI system, software process, machine or other authorized actor capable of economic action.

### Capability
What an actor or system is able to do.

### Capacity
How much of that capability/resource is available or usable at a given time.

### Service
A capability made available for another party's use or benefit.

### Asset
A resource or right capable of storing, producing, enabling, securing or representing value.

### Object
A physical or digital unit that may acquire economic relevance through use, ownership, dependency, repair, resale, recovery, scarcity or transformation.

## 46. No-Waste / Maximum-Use Doctrine

The system should continuously ask:

1. Is this value being used?
2. Is it about to expire?
3. Can another party use it?
4. Can it be repaired?
5. Can it be reused?
6. Can it be transformed?
7. Can it become another service or asset?
8. Can it be returned to a marketplace?
9. Can it become a production input?
10. Can it safely be recovered?

Only after all legitimate options are exhausted should value be classified as non-recoverable residual—and even then the system records why.
