# OMNII — Global Sourcing Capability

**Status:** CANONICAL CAPABILITY SCOPE  
**Scope:** Global-by-default  
**Layer:** Shared ecosystem capability; consumed by Desk, TIP, HAPI World, institutional systems and future products  
**Principle:** Source globally; configure locally; never make one country, supplier directory, marketplace or product the definition of sourcing.

## 1. Constitutional definition

**Global Sourcing** is the reusable ecosystem capability for discovering, qualifying, comparing and arranging access to an existing product, resource, provider, production capability, service, asset, technology, human capability, capital source, infrastructure, route, market or other fulfillable requirement.

Sourcing answers:

> **“Where can we obtain the thing, capability or service required, under the constraints that matter?”**

Sourcing is distinct from Opportunity Discovery. Sourcing primarily begins from a requirement or acquisition intent and searches for ways to satisfy it. Opportunity Discovery primarily begins from an available capability, asset, signal, demand gap or latent possibility and determines what value could be unlocked from it.

## 2. Global-by-default rule

No sourcing domain is constitutionally country-specific.

Geography is represented as context and constraint:

```text
local
→ city / territory
→ national
→ regional
→ continental
→ intercontinental
→ global
```

The engine may prioritize local, regional or global routes according to user intent, economics, risk, law, time, quality, strategic objectives and ecosystem policy. A Nigerian implementation is therefore a jurisdiction configuration, not a separate sourcing ontology. China is a source market, not the architecture.

## 7. Evidence and supplier state

Sourcing records must distinguish discovery from trust.

Minimum evidence states:

```text
DISCOVERED
REPORTED
UNVERIFIED
IDENTITY VERIFIED
CAPABILITY VERIFIED
EVIDENCE VERIFIED
QUALIFIED
SAMPLE/PILOT VERIFIED
PERFORMANCE PROVEN
SUSPENDED
EXPIRED
```

Unknown, unavailable and disputed are valid states.

Evidence must preserve provenance, timestamp/freshness, source, scope and verification method. The system must never infer “verified supplier” solely from a directory listing.

Candidates are compared across relevant dimensions such as:

- total landed cost/value

- specification fit
- capacity
- lead time
- reliability
- evidence confidence
- regulatory fit
- payment terms
- financing availability
- insurance availability
- logistics feasibility
- geographic accessibility
- supplier performance

- environmental factors where measured

The engine should return alternative paths rather than hiding trade-offs behind one opaque score.

## 11. Substitution

A requirement may be satisfied by:

- exact match
- equivalent product
- equivalent service
- alternate manufacturer
- alternate material
- alternate model
- used/refurbished
- local alternative
- alternate technology
- alternate production process
- alternate route
- alternate timing
- alternate financing

Differences must remain visible and acceptance must remain attributable to the authorized decision-maker.

## 12. Sourcing composition

A single requirement may resolve into a composed chain:

```text
requirement
→ design
→ tooling
→ raw material
→ component suppliers
→ manufacturer
→ inspection
→ certification
→ finance
→ insurance
→ logistics
→ customs/compliance
→ delivery
→ acceptance
```

Sourcing orchestrates discovery of the required participants/capabilities but does not absorb the constitutional roles of finance, insurance, customs, transport, marketplaces, payment providers or regulators.

## 14. Reverse and replacement sourcing

Sourcing also responds to changes after an initial selection:

```text
supplier failed
→ identify replacement
→ compare substitute
→ reroute order
→ preserve contractual/evidence state
```

It must also support “source another route” when price, quality, time, capacity or compliance conditions change.

## 16. Failure handling

The system must account for:

- supplier disappearance
- capacity shortage
- MOQ mismatch
- quality failure
- counterfeit/fraud indicators
- delivery delay
- documentation failure
- regulatory rejection
- customs issue
- FX/payment failure
- transport disruption
- force majeure
- inaccurate supplier claims

Failure creates evidence and may trigger replacement sourcing, claims, dispute orchestration or Pulse feedback.

## 17. Sourcing intelligence

The engine may learn from completed transactions:

- on-time performance
- inspection pass/fail
- defect rates
- lead-time variance
- responsiveness
- returns/claims
- price changes
- capacity accuracy
- documentation quality
- performance by product/specification

Reputation remains evidence-based and multi-dimensional; ratings alone are insufficient.

## 18. Regulatory boundary

Global Sourcing may identify requirements and route regulated execution, but it does not become:

- a customs authority
- regulator
- bank
- insurer
- licensed broker
- certification authority
- immigration authority
- legal professional

Jurisdiction-specific rules are attached as regulatory/configuration data and regulated handoffs.

## 21. Non-goals

Global Sourcing does not create:

- a new currency
- a competing marketplace ontology
- a country-specific supplier ontology
- a private regulatory system
- a new legal identity authority
- a new payment rail
- a replacement for TIP

It is the reusable **find-and-qualify access layer**.

## 22. Canonical invariant

> **Sourcing finds the best available means of obtaining what is required. It does not define what the ecosystem considers an opportunity; it discovers sources against a requirement.**
