## 1. Definition

**Management is the continuous capability of directing available resources, people, intelligence, processes, relationships, obligations, risks and change toward intended outcomes under valid authority.**

Management is therefore not synonymous with management title, administration, supervision, ownership, employment, bureaucracy or command. A management function may be performed by a human, team, institution, software, agent, machine, or combination of them, provided authority, accountability and boundaries are explicit.

## 2. Purpose

Management converts intent into coordinated action and converts observed results into the next better state.

```text
INTENT
→ SENSE
→ UNDERSTAND
→ PRIORITIZE
→ PLAN
→ ALLOCATE
→ COORDINATE
→ EXECUTE
→ MONITOR
→ CONTROL
→ REVIEW
→ CORRECT
→ LEARN
→ ADAPT
→ CONTINUE / CLOSE / TRANSFORM
```

The cycle is continuous rather than necessarily linear. Management may enter at any point when an existing state, exception, opportunity or obligation requires intervention.

## 5. Full Management Value Chain

### 5.1 Sense and intake
Capture requests, goals, signals, events, changes, failures, opportunities, obligations and emerging conditions.

### 5.2 Establish authority and scope
Determine who or what is authorized to manage, what is being managed, the boundaries of the mandate, applicable policy, jurisdiction and accountability.

### 5.3 Establish the baseline
Represent the current state, available resources, capabilities, dependencies, performance, commitments, risks, constraints and known unknowns.

### 5.4 Define outcomes
Translate intent into measurable outcomes, priorities, success criteria, non-goals, thresholds and decision conditions.

### 5.5 Diagnose
Identify causes, bottlenecks, gaps, conflicts, dependencies, waste, exposure, opportunities and alternative paths.

### 5.6 Discover and compose
Find existing capabilities, people, agents, assets, providers, information, finance, routes, capacity and workflows before creating anything new.

### 5.7 Plan
Create scenarios, schedules, budgets, resource plans, work plans, dependency plans, risk responses and contingencies.

### 5.8 Decide and prioritize
Rank actions using authority, urgency, value, risk, evidence, constraints, opportunity cost and expected outcome.

### 5.9 Allocate
Assign money, time, people, machines, compute, inventory, capacity, attention and decision authority within permitted limits.

### 5.10 Coordinate
Synchronize people, agents, workflows, vendors, systems, physical operations, communications, handoffs and dependencies.

### 5.11 Execute
Initiate or delegate authorized actions through the Universal Production Fabric and Execution & Integration Fabric.

### 5.12 Monitor and sense again
Continuously observe state, performance, quality, demand, risk, resource consumption and external change.

### 5.13 Control and intervene
Apply approved controls, thresholds, approvals, escalation, throttling, correction, rerouting, replacement, suspension or shutdown.

### 5.14 Resolve exceptions
Classify deviations, incidents, disputes, blocked dependencies, fraud signals, failures and unexpected states; route each to the correct response mechanism.

### 5.15 Verify and assure
Check evidence, compliance, quality, security, delivery, financial integrity and intended-purpose achievement.

### 5.16 Reconcile
Reconcile work, commitments, resources, value, money, inventory, state transitions, evidence and Pulse.

### 5.17 Learn
Capture what worked, what failed, what changed, what was wasteful, what created value and which assumptions were wrong.

### 5.18 Adapt
Change plans, allocations, workflows, capabilities, policies, relationships, interfaces or providers while preserving provenance and authority.

### 5.19 Continue, transform or close
Maintain, scale, federate, migrate, replace, retire or archive the managed object according to lifecycle state.

## 7. Management Modes

### Human-led
Humans make decisions and direct execution.

### Intelligence-assisted
ABBA/agents analyze, recommend, simulate, monitor and prepare actions while humans retain specified authority.

### Delegated
A human or institution grants a defined manager or agent bounded authority for a defined scope and period.

### Automated
Pre-authorized actions execute automatically under explicit policy, thresholds, controls and auditability.

### Joint human-intelligence
Humans and intelligence operate as a managed team with differentiated strengths and explicit handoffs.

### Federated
Multiple managers manage separate scopes while sharing common contracts, dependencies, protocols and state boundaries.

## 10. Decision Management

Every consequential decision SHOULD carry:

- decision identifier;
- subject and scope;
- authority basis;
- relevant evidence;
- alternatives considered;
- constraints;
- expected outcome;
- risk assessment;
- decision maker or delegated authority;
- time validity;
- conditions/thresholds;
- resulting actions;
- review/expiry point;
- actual outcome and learning.

## 11. Resource Management

Resource management covers discovery, valuation, availability, reservation, allocation, utilization, maintenance, replenishment, substitution, pooling, release, recovery and retirement for both tangible and intangible resources.

Scarcity is contextual. The system should seek reuse, pooling, substitution, redistribution and new capacity before assuming that a resource must simply be purchased.

## 23. What Management Is Not

Management is not automatically:

- ownership;
- legal authority;
- employment authority;
- political sovereignty;
- professional licensure;
- banking or custody;
- regulation;
- arbitration or adjudication;
- autonomous command over humans;
- a replacement for domain expertise;
- a guarantee that a managed outcome will succeed.

Those capabilities or authorities remain separate where constitutionally or legally required.

## 25. Core Principle

> **Management is the ecosystem's capability for turning intent and available reality into coordinated movement toward a better state — while preserving authority, evidence, accountability, continuity and learning.**

## 26. Ecosystem Rule

Management is reusable by every product and domain. A product may expose a specialized management experience, but the underlying management semantics, objects, decision model, execution boundary, evidence model and feedback loop belong to the shared ecosystem.
