# CONTINUITY

## Purpose
Continuity governs persistence of Being, Identity, Knowledge, Relationships, Value, Trust, and institutional state through time while permitting legitimate change.

## Canonical Objects
- Continuity record
- State snapshot
- History
- Provenance record
- Lifecycle
- Succession
- Handoff
- Recovery point
- Legacy

## Invariants
1. Required continuity cannot be destroyed without explicit authority and recorded consequence.
2. State changes preserve provenance and temporal ordering.
3. Identity continuity is distinct from state continuity.
4. Recovery must not silently rewrite authoritative history.
5. Succession and handoff preserve required rights, obligations, and context.

## Inputs
Identity, prior state, events, transitions, provenance, lifecycle policy, authority, and recovery requirements.

## Outputs
Current state, historical record, continuity links, recovery state, succession/handoff state, and continuity events.

## Lifecycle
Established → Active → Updated → Suspended → Recovered/Transferred → Retired/Archived.

## Relationships
Continuity preserves the history of Being and Identity, retains Knowledge, records Motion, Value, Trust, and Relationships, and carries execution and composition state across time.

## Dependencies
BEING → IDENTITY → KNOWLEDGE → MOTION → TRUST → RELATIONSHIP → CONTINUITY; VALUE and EXECUTION state may be carried through continuity.

## Composition Rules
Continuity may be composed into products and systems but must remain independently auditable. Composite continuity must retain constituent histories and provenance.

## ABBA
ABBA may monitor continuity, detect breaks, coordinate recovery and handoff, and preserve context within delegated authority. It must not rewrite authoritative history merely to optimize present state.
